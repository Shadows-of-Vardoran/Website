# Beyond Vardoran

## The Return of Magic
<img src="/media/1_S1_Shadows_of_Vardoran_1.3.jpg" alt="World Map" class="w-2/5 max-md:w-full float-right" />

When magic returned to the world, it came like a wound reopening: raw, sudden, and impossible to ignore. From Vardoran, where the Weave first tore itself free and spilled into the world, the resurgence spread outward in violent currents, awakening ancient powers, and dragging buried histories back into the light. Kingdoms that had endured centuries of quiet stagnation found themselves transformed overnight. 

In Balaton, old curses stirred and the ruins began to breathe, watched by hooded figures and things that moved incorrectly. In Sahraan, the return of magic ignited civil war, only to forge the uneasy unity of the Twilight Court from blood, faith, and gold. Veltraxis burned beneath imperial fire as arcanists were chained and cities turned to ash, while Nexarca answered the arcane resurgence with scalpel and steel, birthing horrors of ''logic'' and necromancy in equal measure. Even the frozen reaches of Velgrath were not spared, as the climate changed for the worse and refugees reshaped the land through shared survival. 

Magic's resurgence changed the world for better or worse, stripping away illusions of control and forcing every nation, from empire to ruin, to decide what they would become when the impossible returned.

# The Ruins of Balaton

## The Cursed Legend of Balaton
<img src="/media/balaton_map.jpg" alt="Balaton Map" class="w-3/5 max-md:w-full float-right" />

Legend says the ancient coven of the cursed forest were living adjacent with the Kingdom of Balaton for many years.  Slowly unrest grew within Balaton over the Coven.  Albert, the duke of Balaton who coveted coin and land incited fear over the covens of the forest, greed within his eyes.  The king slowly outlawed the old ways, ostracizing the coven more and more.  When a witch was met by towns folk word spread fast.  Every action watched with bated breath, fear.  One thing was thought to be said by the witch.  One thing perhaps misunderstood, a set of words seen as a curse.  Blame was hurled at the coven yet the coven claimed no one left the forest.  Now convinced with the help of Albert, the old king of Balaton would suffer these forest folk no more.  So the burning commenced, battle took place betwixt the massive old trees.  Spells and swords are said to have flown through the air as flames tempered both forces in blood, char and ash.  The great tree of old which many in the forest coveted was toppled.  In a rage blinded effort to protect what was left of her coven and forest, one of the great Coven leaders, Matka, cast a spell which doomed both sides into oblivion for all eternity.

Some say the witch's curse permeated not just the Cursed Forest but all the land of Balaton too.  Others say Balaton was cursed land before that.  That the king and duke were altered of mind, driven mad by such a dreadful thing.  Either way you look at it the Cursed Forest and the Ruins of Balaton feel connected in the worst ways imaginable.  Rumors say the old king Dracula once attempted to march on the forest and Balaton.  No one knows what happened to the platoon but do you see influence from the dark king there?.

Of course everyone knows this story to have been told from the old tribes of the Oaksong Accord.  Who knows what Balaton’s side of the story was or what really happened.
There’s stories of the Oaksong accord coming together again in the era of entropy but none have been seen together under the banner of Oaksong since.

## Present Day
Present day Balaton rumors from the outside
Nowadays one can catch glimpses of things moving in the ruins.  Some have described animals that don’t look right, see through people that are gone as soon as you blink, shadows which dart away, hooded figures working on something and some hulking beasts stalking the wooded marsh.  Some folk say they’ve heard howling from the dark corners.  Most folk steer clear of the cursed place.

Some hooded figures have come forth saying they’ve gone within the bounds of the Ruins of Balaton.  They claim to have made it their home and have come to be called “The Putrified”.

These folk have a strange air about them.   Never mind that their cloaks don't move like fabric should,  It’s like they aren't even all there or maybe that they are wearing a mask.  Their words are thick with honeyed words causing a clash of uncomfy comfortableness.  Between the lines in what they say seems to lie some truth no one wants to hear.  Something isn't right about them and their strange magic.  It feels old and obscured.

They buy supplies of oddities, animal trapping and skinning, ores and every now and then an item of large monetary value, tell a new and different story before they leave.  Then they whisk away presumably into the ruins of Balaton for months on end.

Normal folk are weary of them yet they tend to get people to talk with them nonetheless with honeyed words or magic tricks.

Trancendum have sent teams into the fringes of the ruins for samples and surveys on where else their waste may be dumped.  They have documented beasts of the ruins and marshes which hold heightened magical mutations including physical, visual, behavioral and defensive.  Exactly what these changes mean has not been found and the group is hesitant to send a sample extraction team back.  There has been no move to realign their dumping grounds thus far.

One must watch one's step and even then, one’s vision cannot always be trusted.  The ground swallows up anyone who dares to step wrong, never to be seen again.

In the east nestled in the base of the mountain range lies Balaton castle in ruins and flooded by the myriad of rivers yet not sunken as far as the west.  The ground holds more solid in this part and the trees are dense shrouding ones vision with a thick canopy of darkness persisting though even day.  Most who come out never made it far in the east ruins.

Those who make it out tell of animals with no visible mutations, just a strange sensation that things aren't right.  The animal will move wrong for the type of animal it is,  the shadow does not move with the body, the creature looking different in the corner of one's eye, lack of noise when the animal moves or sounds of movement when the animal appears to not be moving,  strange occurrences happening around the animal that should not be.  Some have even stated they thought the animals they encountered spoke.  Strange as they are, these animals never seem to leave the bounds of Balaton.  Their actions upon coming to the edge, looking as if they have come to a cliff or wall that they cannot climb nor jump over, usually stalking the border for a while of seeming frustration before slinking out of sight.

Almost all who have come back with this tale say they were hidden by a group of The Hooded who were performing some type of sacrifice or ritual involving the skin of a large beast and that they could tell this gaze that was following them was then more focused on something else.

# Sahraan
## The Civil War & Twilight Court
<img src="/media/sahraan_map.jpg" alt="Sahraan Map" class="w-2/5 max-md:w-full float-right" />

Sahraan was not spared from the unrest that came in on the magic flowing from Vardoran. The simmering tensions between the Sun Courts and the Moon Shrines erupted into civil war. The Sun Courts, with the backing of noble gold, were able to hire mercenaries from the world over. Outfitting them with the finest weapons and armor money could buy, the Sun Courts marched their legions of mercenaries from the cities to raze the villages and scholarly towers of the Moon Shrines and claim that land for Sham’Yara. However, in confidence borne of their faith in the protection of Sham’Yara’s solar light, the Sun Courts’ generals did not count on vampires becoming their biggest obstacle. Though it had long been whispered that vampires conducted dark rituals at The Hollow Eclipse, the march of the Sun Courts confirmed it. 

Emboldened by the sudden increase to their magical abilities, the vampires of The Hollow Eclipse emerged once the Sun Courts’ campaign reached them and disrupted their activities. The forces of the Moon Shrines took advantage and started leveraging vampire attacks to fight back against the Sun Courts. By day, soldiers and mercenaries terrorized the rural population of Sahraan. At night, the vampires would feast, and in the morning the surviving villagers would find the exsanguinated corpses of soldiers and their neighbors alike. 

Terrified citizens began seeking refuge in the Fortress of Oaths as the soldiers of the Sun Courts sacked the villages and the fighters of the Moon Shrines followed the vampires to the cities. The refugees, worshippers of Sham’Yara and Nakri’el alike, began to wonder why they must suffer the sting of the blade by the light of day and the bite of the fang in the shadow of the moon. Why could they not live in harmony as Sham’Yara and Nakri’el did in the beginning? 

From this was born the Twilight Court: The union of the day and the night. Sympathetic deserters from both the Sun Courts and the Moon Shrines, as well as vampires who wished to go back to their relative isolation to be left to conduct occult research in peace, joined the refugees to oppose both the Sun Courts and the Moon Shrines. As the Twilight Court earned more victories and provided aid to the areas devastated by the civil war, it gained favor with Sahraan’s people. Eventually, it gained enough popular support to declare the Fortress of Oaths the new seat of government in Sahraan. 

The Sun Courts and the Moon Shrines as they had been were abolished. The new government, which kept the name Twilight Court, also abolished the Sun Court’s nobility and held elections for governors in the cities. It established the scholarly towers of the former Moon Shrines as official universities. The Hollow Eclipse was turned over to the vampires that had assisted the Twilight Court. It was decided that Sahraan would be governed by a council of three elected delegates: A worshipper of Sham’Yara, a worshipper of Nakri’el, and a vampire from The Hollow Eclipse.

In the centuries since the civil war, the Twilight Court has had to maintain vigilance to quell uprisings from supporters of the old order and their descendants.

## Religion and Culture

The dual gods Sham’Yara and Nakri’el are still worshipped in Sahrann, but no longer as opposing forces. Instead, they are viewed by the Twilight Court as two sides of one coin. The currency has changed from gold to electrum, an alloy of gold and silver, to represent the reunion of day and night. The coins are stamped with the faces of the Twilight Court’s elected delegates at the time they were minted. Supporters of the Sun Courts and the Moon Shrines as they existed prior to the civil war still exist, and keep secret enclaves where they practice the old traditions.

## Return of Simmering Tensions

The tensions in Sahraan have changed from being between the Sun Courts and the Moon Shrines to being between the Twilight Court and the descendants of the disenfranchised nobility and those that still practice the old beliefs, collectively referred to as the Traditionalists. The Traditionalists periodically engage in skirmishes and uprisings across the country, trying to reestablish a foothold. The Twilight Court swiftly destroys Traditionalist enclaves whenever they are found, but despite its best efforts it has not been able to stamp out the Traditionalists. It is only a matter of time before Sahraan experiences another civil war. 

## Locations
### Fortress of Oaths
Built from sun-baked stone and moon-forged steel, it’s a holy site where pacts are sworn and sealed with blood and gold. Being the new capital of Sahraan the city borders have expanded leading to several new defensive towers and walls being built to defend its expanding population. During the civil war tents and coverings were made as temporary housing for those who came for sanctuary. Most have been taken down but some still stay in their makeshift lodgings believing it to be a safer home then returning to a town destroyed from war. 

### The Mirrored Sands
A shimmering desert is said to have formed from Sham’Yara’s tears during the eclipse. At noon, it blinds the eyes—only those with true intent can cross. Through the civil war battles would occur along the edges of the shimmering desert. The sands covered with weapons and armor, the bones of the brave warriors stick out from the sand, and the ashes of vampires make this area of once shimmering sand into a wasteland of junk where even the gods turn a blind eye to the devastating remains.

### The Hollow Eclipse
A ruinous crater temple where Nakir’el was said to have cast his first curse. The Hollow Eclipse through the years is publicly known to hold vampiric rituals and for being filled consistently with a large portion of Sahraan's vampiric population

### Cteji
The current largest city of Sahraan beating even larger then the Fortress of oaths and Hidand combined. A large wall wraps around the city with only two gates allowing entrance in and out, both hold guards who inspect and review everyone who goes through. Despite the inspections Cteji is the most traveled through city letting nearly anyone inside, leading to some calling the gate inspections a farce

### Scholarly Towers
The scholarly towers of the moon shrines after becoming universities become highly sought after to be a part of their Alumnus. While no tower is actively avoided the least prestigious is known as Shayla having a lower graduate rate and the least amount of successful students compared to the rest.

### Bidameihr
The most prestigious university in Sahraan, it is also highly selective on who it takes in, only having a fraction of total students compared to other universities. Those who graduate are highly sought after and are at the forefront of advancements in Sahraan.

### Shanty Towns
During the civil war shanty towns were made in mass littering the country. Refugees tried to scrounge up whatever home and normalcy they could in the midst of the war. Many shanty towns were demolished and raided the same as every other town in Sahraan. However the Refugees continued to persist despite the hardships attempting to make defenses out of the environment given to them. Once the civil war ended Shanty towns were slowly demolished as cities were built back up once again, many still remain however as the scars of war are as prevalent as ever.

### Hidand
The largest of these Shanty towns is known as Hidand. It holds the majority of the refugee population as well as a market for both legal and illegal trading to take place. Homes here have evolved from crude tents into makeshift homes out of stone and other easy to mold materials.

### Qahashn Sand Tunnels
Most call it a hoax however there have been several accounts of short underground tunnels. The accounts also state that they've seen individuals going in and out of them under the cover of dark.

# VELGRATH
## Northern Asylum
<img src="/media/velgrath_map.jpg" alt="Velgrath Map" class="w-2/5 max-md:w-full float-right" />

Given its relative isolation in the north and the necessity of mutual reliance for survival in the cold climate, Velgrath was spared from much of the upheaval that affected the other countries of the world as magic spread from Vardoran. It was only with the awakening of a dormant beast in the northern mountains that Velgrath knew more frost than they thought possible. The awakening of the Frost Leviathan resulted in rivers that had flowed year-round now freeze solid for months at a time, their beds locked beneath blue-white stone. The upper northern mountains have become deathly still, their passes choked by eternal frost and shrieking winds that flay flesh within minutes. Exiles of the Velgrathian population flee towards it's domain, it is unknown what happens to these unfortunate souls.

Velgrath became a destination for refugees desperate enough to brave the rough northern seas in search of safety from the widespread unrest wrought by the return of magic. The tribes of Velgrath accepted those that made it to Velgrath's shores with open arms, integrating them into their villages, and honoring those the refugees lost in the journey. 

Slowly, the villages of Velgrath were built up with the unique expertise and abilities the refugees brought from their home countries. Small shrines to the gods of the refugees began to appear. Food began to take on the flavors of other countries. Velgrath's exports came to include unique medicines created from the fusion of Velgrath tradition and the cultural knowledge of the refugees and furs with unique designs embossed on them. 

With the refugees also came mining and smelting knowledge, with which the tribes of Velgrath began utilizing the rich ore veins of the land. Metal, previously scarce, was used to improve tools. It was also used to forge weapons, as the tribesmen of Velgrath knew from the tales of the refugees that trouble was likely headed for its shores.

# The Deadlands

## End of the Era of Entropy
<img src="/media/deadlands_map.jpg" alt="Deadlands Map" class="w-2/7 float-right" />

The Deadlands, just like the rest of the globe, felt the effects of the end of the last era. The influx of magic fed the Lich King; revitalizing the storm of necromancy and the vicious cycle of undeath that holds the Deadlands in its grasp. The land became even more inhospitable. The dead rose more frequently, and in greater numbers than before. No longer devoid of magic, the Lich King’s movements have become frenzied, faster, and far less predictable.


## The Necrotic Leviathan
The Lich King no longer wanders the Deadlands alone. The Necrotic Leviathan wanders the wastes, following the Lich King and feasting off the necrotic magic it leaves behind.

The people of the Deadlands believe that the Necrotic Leviathan has always been there; merely slumbering beneath the soil while the land was starved of magic. Some claim to have seen it, the day the last era ended, when it emerged from the soil and gave a great and terrible cry. It was as it awoke that the Lich King doubled in size and began to erratically move about the Deadlands.

Worship in the Deadlands has shifted to include the Necrotic Leviathan along with the Lich King. Those Bound to the Necrotic Leviathan serve it with a fervor; dragging living sacrifices into the Lich King’s path, ensuring that they are consumed and, in turn, their master is fed.


## The Stalwart and Scavengers
The villages at the edges of the Deadlands remain, despite the Lich King’s increasingly unpredictable movements. The Stalwart border villages hunker in the valleys of the nearby mountains, focused only on survival. There is precious little by way of government in the Stalwart villages, as the fight for survival leaves little room for much else.

Those who dare to venture beyond the borders are the Scavengers. They seek treasure, magic, and items from a history long buried. Some seek a way to kill the Lich King, while others simply seek to understand its true nature.

## Processions & the Cult of the Necrotic Leviathan
Others live within rolling villages– called Processions– and move daily, making them difficult to locate. They brave the heart of the Deadlands itself, relying on the Necromancy Leviathan to avoid the Lich King’s path. They boast rolling huts and ramshackle landships. The Bound of the Necrotic Leviathan decorate their buildings with corpses and rocks to better blend in with the desolate landscape they call home. You may not see them coming until they are upon you.

Processions raid border villages for sacrifices to feed to the Lich King, ensuring that their master is fed in-turn, and they remain in the Leviathan’s Favor. Processions have been known to capture unlucky refugees from the holy war in Frankleburg. Crossing the Deadlands is more dangerous than ever before, as travelers must be wary of both the Lich King and the Leviathan in its shadow, *and* their fanatical followers.

# Veltraxis
## The Ashfall of Veltraxis
<img src="/media/veltraxis_map.jpg" alt="Veltraxis Map" class="w-2/5 max-md:w-full float-right" />

When the dormant veins of magic awoke once more, Veltraxis, once the jewel of the western Lands, barely had time to raise its banners before they were drowned in flame.
The return of magic did not herald an age of wonder.  It heralded conquest.

The Empire of Dreughardt struck within days of the Weave’s resurgence.  
Empress **Vanessa Dreughardt**, wielding both divine rhetoric and unrelenting steel, declared the republic’s arcanists heretics unworthy of the “gift they squandered.”  
Her armies marched through the mountain passes and descended upon Veltraxis like a cleansing fire.

The **Glass Citadel**, heart of the Republic and home to the Senate of Five, fell within three days. Its crystalline towers refracted cannonlight until they burst into molten shards that rained across the river-vales.  
The Citadel’s libraries burned for a week, the smoke blackening the skies for miles.

What was not burned was _taken_.  
Thousands of mages, scholars, and apprentices were seized alive, chained, and branded with the sigil of the Iron Flame. Those who resisted were executed en masse. Their ashes were mixed into the mortar of new Imperial forts, “so that the soil would remember obedience.”

All by decree of the Empress.

## The Division of the Republic

When the fires finally dimmed, half of Veltraxis was gone, its river cities reduced to smoldering wastelands and hollow aqueducts filled with the dead.  
The surviving territories were divided in two:

- **The Eastern Protectorate**, claimed outright by the Empire and placed under the rule of **Prince Valeerin Dreughardt**, a distant cousin of Vanessa, charged with “civilizing the fractured minds of the scholar class.”
    
- **The Western Ashlands**, designated a **Colony of Containment**, where prisoners of war, heretical magi, and surviving Veltraxians were confined indefinitely.
    

The **Prince’s Mandate** was to restore productivity to the Protectorate through “measured reeducation.” Temples were erected atop burned universities, and the people were taught new prayers.

The Ashlands, however, were left to rot.  
Imperial overseers patrol the ruins to monitor.  
The remnants of Veltraxis’s population remain there: broken scholars, crippled artificers, and their descendants live in perpetual servitude. Forced to sort through the ashes of their old civilization to recover anything “of potential Imperial utility.”

## The Colony (Ashlands)

The Colony sprawls across the charred basin where the Glass Citadel once stood. The very ground hums faintly with residual mana, unstable and poisonous.  
To survive, the inhabitants built their shelters from the melted crystal and burnt stone of their fallen capital, the so-called _Shard Hovels_.

They are watched by an imperial order, charged with ensuring that “no spark of false enlightenment rekindles.”  
The Order’s Crusaders enforce curfews, burn heretical speech, and administer weekly purges of forbidden texts.

Every few years, a shipment of prisoners from the Empire arrives. Scholars, mages, or suspected heretics to be added to the Colony’s labor force. None ever leave.

## The Eastern Protectorate of Veltraxis

The Protectorate’s surface shines. Rebuilt cities with straight Imperial roads, banners of the Iron Flame flying above once-arcane academies now converted into military colleges.  
But beneath the marble and steel lies a people without a future.

Education is rationed, history rewritten. Children are taught that their ancestors were “sorcerous traitors saved by the Empress’s mercy.”  
The imperial clergy controls all scholarly work; experimentation is allowed only if it serves the war effort or the Empire's will.

Prince Roderic Dreughardt, current ''ruler'' of the protectorate, rules suffocatingly efficient. He hosts public lectures on loyalty, commissions statues of the Empress in conquered plazas, and funds “purity festivals” to celebrate the extinction of Veltraxis’s old faith. Though rumor has it that disdain can be viewed if only for a split second upon his face at each of these acts.

# Nexarca
## Magic Returning
<img src="/media/nexarca_map.jpg" alt="Nexarca Map" class="w-2/5 max-md:w-full float-right" />

When magic began to stir once more across the fractured continents, the Dominion did not celebrate. It _mobilized._

To the Central Logic Assembly, this resurgence was not a divine gift but a once-in-a-lifetime _anomaly_; the perfect moment to dissect the irrational force that had dominated human civilization for centuries. The return of magic represented the ultimate unsolved variable, and therefore, the most valuable research opportunity in recorded history.

Across Nexarca’s grey spires, the Scientific Party entered what is now called the **Hyperactive Phase**-- a period of feverish intelligence gathering and state-sanctioned abduction.

Those displaying rekindled magical potential were quietly seized by **Entropy Directorate operatives** and placed into Containment Cells for dissection and observation. The Assembly studied mana flow and the neurological patterns of spell invocation, often destroying their subjects in the process.

Where other nations saw miracles, Nexarca saw infection.  
Where others rebuilt their mage towers, Nexarca constructed morgues for the metaphysical.

They watched the world tremble as elemental storms broke all over and strange aetheric creatures returned to the wastes. They watched with analytical satisfaction as the once-great magical empire were crushed beneath the marching boots of the **Dreughardt Empire**.

And in that moment, cold logic reached its inevitable conclusion:  
_When the Empire’s zeal was done with gods and mages, it would turn its gaze to reason itself._

## The Technomancy Initiative

The logical conclusion of war coming soon began the Dominion’s most ambitious project: the _Mechanization of Death._

The Central Logic Assembly decreed that mortality, emotion, and inefficiency must all be made obsolete. If death could be harnessed, it could be perfected.

From this doctrine emerged **Project Technomancy**, a grotesque hybrid of necromancy and machinecraft. Corpses were wired with conductive sinew, organs replaced by chemical engines, and decayed bone fused with alloys.

The Dominion would make war as it made industry: tireless, methodical, and precise.

Yet the results were abominable. The furnaces belched black smoke as twisted cadavers crawled from operating tables, screaming through metal mouths before collapsing. The laboratories of the Technomantic Division became charnel factories of innovation, and the halls echoed with both scientific triumph and the stench of burning flesh.

But the Assembly did not cease.  
Failure was merely _inefficient progress._

## The Empire’s Advance

When the Dreughardt Empire delivered its declaration of war, the Dominion’s response was deliberate inaction. To the Assembly, _not responding_ was a calculated decision: without formal acknowledgement, the Empire’s campaign would be seen as an act of aggression without legitimacy. The goal was time. To buy every possible second for their research to reach completion.

But logic met zeal, and zeal cared little for *logic.*

The Empire’s Iron Flame descended upon the eastern border without warning or order. Knight commanders and crusaders carved through Nexarcan defenses with divine fury, their prayers echoing against the walls of steel citadels.

For an entire year, Nexarca bled.  
Entire provinces fell in flames. Factory-cities were razed. Populations were culled or converted beneath the Empire’s banner. Nexarcan militia were no match for the disciplined brutality of the crusaders.

The Dominion recorded each defeat dispassionately.  Every lost city a data point, every massacre a statistic. But even numbers have limits.

## The Bloom Breakthrough

It was **Avery Bloom**, Head Researcher of the Technomancy Project, who altered the course of the war.

Her first working prototype of the **Necrosis Engine** proved that the human nervous system could be overridden entirely with mechanical stimulus.  
A corpse could walk again. Not by will or soul, but by electrical command...granted there be interaction with Necromancy.

By the second year of the war, Nexarca’s foundries no longer produced mere machines. They birthed the **Technonecrotic Infantry**:  
Mechanical cadavers clad in armor of their own bones and Hyperkeratosis, directed by the Logic Centers deep beneath Virelic Spire.

These soldiers did not sleep, did not starve, and did not drown.  
They marched tirelessly through swamp, marsh, and mire: environments where Imperial steel rusted and Imperial flesh grew cold.

Where cavalry could not charge, the dead advanced.  
Where supply lines failed, they continued to move.

Even greater horrors followed. The Dominion unveiled **Siege Beasts**, the product of anatomical synthesis. Mutated creatures combining the bone density of bulls, the mass of elephants, and the metabolism of ruminants. Fed a chemical slurry that caused volatile bloating, and fitted with ignition ports upon their snouts, these monstrosities exhaled flames through their own decomposition.

The Empire called them _Hellbeasts._  
The Assembly logged them as _Unit Type 47-B: Bio-Combustive Ordinance Platforms._

## The Turning Tide

The war’s first major confrontation, the **Battle of Ered Vallis**,  nearly annihilated Nexarca outright.  
The Empire’s crusaders shattered the steel front, killing over 400,000 Nexarcans in three months. The Dominion lost twelve entire provinces before the Logic Assembly enacted **Operation Correction**: the full mobilization of Technonecrotic assets.

What followed defied even their models.

The Empire’s own dead, reanimated by Bloom’s designs, rose to meet the Empire’s advance. For the first time in history, the faithful faced the resurrected remnants of their own slaughter.

Panic spread through the crusader lines. Soldiers saw their brothers among the ranks of the enemy: blank-faced, driven by logic alone.  
The once-gilded fields of Ered Vallis became a mire of ash and mechanized corpses, eternally grinding against one another beneath storms of black powder and rain.

The Empire still held its edge in open terrain and forest warfare, where cavalry and command could flourish. Yet Nexarca’s dominance in night battles, trench sieges, and swamps halted the seemingly unstoppable crusade. 

The war ended with the death of the Empress, who sacrificed herself to take out over half of the Scientific Party that led Nexarca.

## Aftermath

When **Empress Elidra Dreughardt** fell in her siege upon the Nexarcan Logic Assembly, the explosion that consumed the fortress did not just annihilate an army, it also amputated the Dominion’s mind.

The **Logic Assembly**, seat of the Scientific Party and nucleus of all state governance, was buried beneath molten metal and vaporized steel. Over half of the **Senior Researchers**, including entire records of Theoreticals, were vaporized in the blast. The detonation ruptured entire data archives and erased a century of schematics, experimentation logs, and artificial computation formulas.

The Dominion’s intelligence grid, once the most advanced network of information ever constructed, went dark for **forty nine hours**. During that time, no machine responded, no Overseer acknowledged input, and no Directive was issued. Nexarca, a state built on perfect process, had entered its first true moment of chaos.

Within the ruins of the Assembly, a handful of surviving Party members clawed their way through the ash-choked corridors, recovering fragments of charred data tablets and melted archives. Among them was **Avery Bloom**, half-blinded and with her lungs burned from the explosion, who led the emergency reactivation of the Central Core’s secondary processors. Her actions restored minimal operational capacity to the Dominion, preventing full systemic collapse.

But the cost was unimaginable.

Without the central governing algorithms and much of the Scientific Party’s higher command, local Overseers defaulted to self-preservation protocols. Entire cities sealed themselves off, their mechanical governance reverting to automated rationing and defensive isolation. For months, the Dominion existed as a network of disconnected enclaves, each calculating survival independently, a machine nation fragmented into a thousand cold thoughts.

The Empire, leaderless and bleeding, withdrew to regroup in exhaustion. For the first time, the Dreughardt legions marched _away_ from victory.

Nexarca, meanwhile, lay crippled yet unbroken.

## The Reconstruction of Logic

In the months following the detonation, what remained of the Scientific Party convened. From there, the surviving architects of reason began the slow process of rebuilding their shattered dominion.

Avery Bloom, now elevated to **Acting Coordinator of the Assembly**, took personal charge of reestablishing the Dominion’s core protocols. Her face had been half-reconstructed with technonecrotic grafts; her voice permanently distorted by respiratory scarring. But her intellect remained sharp as diamond.

Under her direction, the Dominion enacted the **Continuity Protocol**; an emergency directive mandating that no single facility, city, or directorate would ever again contain all critical data. Instead, research and governance would be decentralized, with redundant backups and parallel logic cores distributed across the entire nation.

Reconstruction was slow, but methodical. 

And all the while, the Dominion’s war machines continued their silent patrols across the ruins of the Iserith Platea, mindless sentinels following old orders long after the war had officially ended. Entire valleys became graveyards of both man and mechanism, where Imperial armour rusted beside Nexarcan pylons.

Only when the Dominion’s systems were sufficiently stabilized did its armies begin their slow, relentless reclamation of territory. There were no grand offensives, no triumphant charges. Just methodical reoccupation.

City by city, the Dominion reactivated its industries.  
Town by town, the logic cores hummed again.  
The corpses of fallen soldiers, Imperial and Nexarcan alike, were retrieved for reconstruction.

By the end of the decade, Nexarca had reclaimed most of its lost provinces. Its population, drastically reduced, was supplemented by **Synthetic Labor Units** built from the remnants of both sides.

# Empire of Dreughardt
## Empress Vanessa’s remaining rule
<img src="/media/dreughardt_map.jpg" alt="Dreughardt Map" class="w-2/5 max-md:w-full float-right" />

With the long-dormant currents of magic returning to the world, the Dreughardt Empire struck before their rivals could regain their former might. Their first target was **Veltraxis**, a neighbour known for its gifted arcanists and rebellious republics. Within four days of magic’s resurgence, the Empire’s banners darkened the horizon, the cleansing had begun.

Led personally by **Empress Vanessa Dreughardt**, the campaign was merciless. Children were seized to be “reborn as loyal subjects,” while their parents were executed as they were _beyond salvation_. Those who dared wield magic in defiance had their hands severed and were marched away in chains. Veltraxis fell swiftly. A loyal noble was installed to rule under imperial decree, and what remained of the republic was declared a colony. Proof, Vanessa claimed, of the Empire’s “mercy to the obedient.”

Veltraxis’s capital became a warning. Magic was outlawed under threat of public execution, and even the utterance of the Empress’s name was forbidden to its people. The message was clear: obedience brings order. Rebellion, erasure.

But Vanessa’s ambition did not rest. Her gaze turned to **Nexacra**, the technoratic dominion to the south. When the Empire’s demand for surrender was met only with silence, she ordered a full invasion. The campaign lasted two bloody years. Nexacra’s abominations, half-living corpses, fused flesh with machinery, and beasts of stitched sinew, clashed endlessly with the Empire’s armored knights and sacrificial battalions.

The largest siege upon a Nexarcian Fortress marked the war’s end. As the Empress pressed the assault, Nexacra unleashed one of its hidden horrors, their ace in the hole. In the chaos, the creature broke through the Imperial lines, threatening her command post. 

Witnesses claim that Vanessa herself intervened to shield her only son, Prince Kaldric, from the beast’s advance. Her sword broke, her armor cracked, and her blood soaked the stones before her soldiers could react. The abomination ran from the battlefield, injuried greatly, much to the shame of the Imperial army. The Empress fell, her final breath spent protecting the heir she had forged for war.

Her death sent shockwaves through the Empire. The soldiers withdrew, carrying her broken banner home. From that day, the Dreughardt line swore eternal vengeance against Nexacra, ''The blight to the south.''

## Next Emperors/Empresses

### Emperor Kaldric Dreughardt

**Title:** _The Iron Regent_ 

#### Background

Kaldric Dreughardt, first and only son of Empress Vanessa, was born in conquest and raised in discipline. Trained from childhood to embody his mother’s will, he learned that mercy was weakness and unity was sacred.

When Vanessa perished in Nexarca, Kaldric seized command and declared himself Emperor. “The Empire does not bleed,” he proclaimed, standing before the fallen standard of his mother’s legions. His ascension marked the end of Vanessa’s crusade of fire and the dawn of an empire forged in iron.

#### Reign

- **Forged the Iron Legions**, standing armies where every citizen was a soldier first, tradesman second. The Empire became an organism of discipline, each man and woman a cell in its unyielding body.
    
- **Founded the Council of Steel**, composed of generals, tacticians, and philosophers tasked with perfecting the human form of warfare through perfection to crush Nexarca.
    
- **Launched the Great Culling of Relics**, ordering every magical artifact confiscated or destroyed to prevent what he called “the rot of reliance.”
    
- **Abolished noble crests**, replacing them with the Sigil of The Empire. A blazing emblem symbolizing vigilance and dominion without gods or sorcery.
    
- Enforced a law demanding every citizen drill weekly with arms. To live under Kaldric’s rule was to serve; to serve was to exist. And to exist, one must fight for that right.
   

#### Death & Legacy

Emperor Kaldric Dreughardt died during a military parade, felled by a stroke mid-speech.

He is remembered as the son who turned grief into law, who transmuted his mother’s dream of conquest into a doctrine of unyielding discipline. Under Kaldric, the Dreughardt name ceased to be merely royal, it became synonymous with order itself.

The Empire longer burned with Vanessa’s passion; it endured with Kaldric’s iron.

### Empress Elidra Dreughardt

**Title:** _The Tempered Flame_  

#### Background

Born to Emperor Kaldric Dreughardt, Elidra was raised amidst the clangor of forges and the recitation of discipline. Unlike her father, whose reign had choked all warmth from the Empire, Elidra understood that control required not only fear but faith. She believed the Empire could not rely on suppression alone; it needed to _believe_ in its endurance, to worship its order as sacred truth.

Upon ascending the throne after her father’s death, she declared that “the Empire must not only _endure_, but _inspire endurance_.” Her reign sought to refine Vanessa’s passion and Kaldric’s severity into a single tempered creed, one that could burn without consuming itself.

But her rule also inherited her bloodline’s ancient hatred: the memory of her grandmother’s death at Nexarca, at the hands of Nexarca’s necrotech abominations. Elidra’s vision of a perfected Empire demanded retribution.

#### Reign

- Taught that true order is not blind obedience but controlled resilience. Under this creed, the Empire’s citizens were taught to view endurance as both a civic and spiritual virtue, ''the unbreakable flesh of the Iron Sun.”
    
- **Founded the Order of the Iron Flame**, a militant clergy of philosophers, strategists, and scholars who replaced priests. They preached that service to the Empire was the highest act of faith, and that loyalty itself was divine.
    
    
- **Reformed military academies**, introducing the _Three Pillars of Command_: Knowledge, Resolve, and Silence. Cadets studied rhetoric, philosophy, and history. 
    
- **Declared the Second Purge of Nexarca**, a campaign of vengeance disguised as “preemptive defense.” Elidra’s armies razed the borderlands, torching entire biolabs and enslaving captured technocrats to dissect their “blasphemous machinery.”
    
- **Commissioned the Memorial Wall**, an enormous fortification raised upon the ruins where Vanessa fell. Its inscription reads: _“Here the Empire remembers, and never forgives.”_
    

#### Death & Legacy

Elidra Dreughardt met her end during a siege upon a Nexarcan Logic Assembly building, deep in Nexarcan territory. When the building was overrun by the enemy’s bone-forged beasts, she refused retreat. Accounts claim she ignited the fortress powder stores herself, annihilating both her legions and the enemy host in a single blinding inferno. 

Her remains were never recovered; only her saber, found molten and fused into the blackened stone of the building. At the cost of her life, Elidra took out over half of the Scientific Party members, resulting in a cripling of Nexarca's governing capabilities. 

Elidra’s death became legend, a parable of endurance through destruction. Her reign is remembered as the Empire’s most _devotional_ era: less tyranny than crusade, less iron than burning faith. She transformed the Dreughardt legacy into a living religion of resilience.

Her words, _“The Empire refines itself through suffering,”_ are carved above the Gate of Loyalty in the capital, a passage every soldier walks through before war, and every corpse returns through after it.

### Empress Selvaria Dreughardt II (Current Empress)

**Title:** _The Iron Flame Reborn_  

#### Background

Daughter of **Empress Elidra Dreughardt**, Selvaria was born into an Empire already weighed down by ghosts. From her earliest years, she was taught that hesitation was treason, compassion was corrosion, and that her bloodline had been chosen to keep the world from collapsing into weakness.

But the shadow of her forebears loomed vast: **Vanessa the Iron Flame**, **Kaldric the Iron Regent**, **Elidra the Tempered Flame**. Each immortalised in marble, scripture, and steel. Selvaria grew convinced that only through conquest could she match their legacy.

When her mother perished in the inferno at the Logic Assembly building, the throne fell to Selvaria at the age of twenty-one. The High Legates whispered she was too young, too emotional, yet she silenced them all in her first speech:

> “My ancestors burned so we would not forget how. I will make the world remember the fire.”

What began as a vow of inheritance soon turned into obsession.

#### **Reign**

- **Declared the Wars of Resurgence**, a series of escalating invasions along the southern front, aimed at reclaiming territories once lost during her grandmother’s reign. Officially framed as “protective restorations of rightful imperial soil,” they are, in truth, wars of vindication. Fought to prove that the Dreughardt flame has not dimmed.
    
- **Reinstituted the Doctrine of Absolute Order**, first proclaimed by Vanessa herself. Under this law, public mourning and dissent are banned. To grieve is to doubt the Empire’s eternal strength; to doubt is to rust.
    
- **Formed the Wardens of Flame**, an expanded inquisition charged with policing loyalty, rumor, and even _thought_. 
    
- **Established the Children’s Regiments**, state academies that seize war orphans and raise them as loyal tacticians, riflemen, and officers. They chant Vanessa’s battle hymns before sleep, their lullabies replaced with war songs.
    
- **Rebuilt the March of Iron**, a grand annual procession through the capital in which the Empress personally inspects the legions by torchlight. The ceremony, intended to rekindle unity, has become a fevered spectacle of fanatic devotion.

### Prince Roderic Dreughardt (Current Heir)

A quiet, analytical 19 year old man, Roderic has mastered both the rifle and the art of command. His writings circulate secretly among the academies, treatises on _“human perfection through discipline, not fear.”_

While publicly loyal, he has begun to question his mother’s holy obsession with her lineage. To his inner circle he has murmured:

> “If we build our thrones from ashes, what will we sit upon when the fire dies?”

Despite this, the young legions adore him. 

## Current State of the Empire

Under Selvaria’s reign, the Empire once more trembles with the sound of marching boots and the thunder of rifles. The banners of the **Iron Flame** rise over the reclaimed ruins of old wars, and her legions chant the names of the dead as blessings.

Yet behind the spectacle lies fragility. The Empress grows increasingly paranoid, haunted by whispers that she is _unworthy_ of the Dreughardt line: a pale imitation of Vanessa’s wrath, Kaldric’s steel, and Elidra’s endurance.

Her wars have not yet broken the Empire, but neither have they crowned it victorious. Every pyre she lights burns another piece of herself.

The Empire has been recouping it's strength and zeal after the major loss at the last Great War within Nexarcan grounds. Though the Empress does not wish for her army *nor* the enemy to rest, assassination attempts and consistent scouting parties into Nexarcan territory never stopped.

## Blightmoor

Before the river had a name, before the first stones of the Necropolis were carved, the world was not ruled by gods, but by cycles. Heat and cold. Birth and rot. The great turning wheel of existence. But from the wheel’s eternal motion came two divine sparks; one seeking to illuminate, the other to understand the dark between the spokes.

Thus were born Ignariel and Nymeris. They were not enemies, but reflections. Siblings, according to some cults. Lovers, according to older scrolls. Two deific forces who together maintained the cycle of life and death: Ignariel kindled the flame of birth, Nymeris folded it into silence when its time had come.
For uncounted ages, there was peace. But mortals, as they often do, broke the balance.

As empires rose and fell upon the bones of forgotten ages, humanity began to fear death, rather than embrace it. They worshipped the sun, begged it to hold back the night. In time, Ignariel’s radiance grew stronger through devotion, and with it, his pride.
Nymeris was not hated--at first. She was still revered in funeral rites and whispers, her name etched onto tombstones and sung in lullabies. But she was not loved as her brother was. Her temples faded. Her statues were buried. And slowly, she withdrew from the waking world.
Ignariel, seeing the adoration he received, sought to burn away what remained of her domain. He commanded temples be raised in his name. He promised eternal life through faith, a flame that would never go out. To many, it was salvation. To Nymeris, it was betrayal.
In silence, she retreated to the Veil Beyond, taking with her the secrets of rest, of peace, of dreams without waking. Where once the gods had walked as twin keepers of a cycle, now they stood apart--sun and shadow, divided by flame.

It is said that Blightmoor began as a gift of reconciliation.
The ancient city that would become Blightmoor was founded upon a pact: the Brotherhood of Nymeris, necromancers and memory-keepers, would build a sanctuary where death was preserved, not destroyed, and where the divine balance between light and night could be honored once more.
Ignariel agreed, reluctantly. His priests built sunlit sanctuaries above, even as the Brotherhood hollowed out chambers deep within the Twinkeep Mountains. The Solar Basilica and the Necropolis of Nymeris were constructed simultaneously, each tethered to the same spiritual heart, but facing in opposite directions.; Above, life was celebrated.
