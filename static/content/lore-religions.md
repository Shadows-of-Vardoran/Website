# Religions

## Qariel
<details open><summary>Click to expand/collapse Qariel</summary>
  
**The Flame that Feeds Itself - They who shape desire.**
<div>
Qariel is not the god of any one domain. Qariel is the god of more. More blood. More devotion. More harvest. More steel, flesh, sound, hunger, gold, fire, purpose. To worship Qariel is to never stop, to never be satisfied, to sacrifice selfhood in the pursuit of perfection or indulgence--until that very pursuit devours the soul, leaving behind only a hollow echo that screams “again.”
</div>
<div>
A wailing choir of flayed monks, carving prayers into themselves with bone needles. 
A demon forged of molten teeth and gold, laughing as it eats warhorses and it's riders. 
A perfectly silent potter, who remakes the same clay bowl ten thousand times in a dark cell. 
A king who never stops building, whose city is a spiral of towers too tall for light to reach. 
</div>
<div>
Worship is not tied to what is done. Only that it is done again, and again, and again, until nothing else remains. Though daemons and violent cults are the most visible (and feared) of Qariel’s followers, they are far from the only ones. 
</div>
<div>
The god's true cult is fractured, a collection of the obsessed and the devoted, all pursuing excess in different forms. The Daemons however remain the most prevalent, Demon summoning is as much a talent as it is a teachable skill. The abilities of individual demon summoners vary on a number of factors, but the primary two are the amount of magic available and the disposition of the summoner. Summoners typically do not gain more skill throughout their life, though they may find their abilities improve, worsen, or change as their personality changes. 
</div>
<div>
Experienced summoners and mages often can tell how talented a new summoner will be simply by getting a sense of their personality. Of course the amount of available magic is also vitally important. The act of summoning a demon is destructive and consumes magic and life force equal to the demon's strength. It is possible to begin a ritual that will only complete once the requisite magic has been consumed. These daemons grow stronger as mortal magic flourishes. When a mage dies near a summoning circle, the lines blur and Qariel’s beasts may take the body as their own, warping bone, voice, and thought into a vessel of excess. 

No corpse lies still in the presence of a Daemon, after all.
</div>
</details>

## Ignariel 
<details open><summary>Click to expand/collapse Ignariel</summary>
  
**The Undying Flame**

<div>
  
**Domain:** Life, Light, Order, Purity
**Symbol:** A haloed flame within a sunburst
**Worshipped in:** The East (Brightmoor), especially in the Gleaming Ward
</div>
<div>
  
**Overview:**
Ignariel is the radiant deity of fire, birth, and unyielding vitality. Once one half of a balanced divine pair, he became ascendant through the fervent devotion of mortals who feared death. With each prayer and temple raised in his name, his pride swelled. He promised eternal life through flame and faith, seeking to banish death as a flaw in creation. His followers are warriors, zealots, and preservers of the flesh—clad in gold and ivory, devoted to order, and opposed to the decay of mortality.
</div>
<div>
  
**Doctrine:**
Death is an imperfection to be purged.
Eternal life is earned through devotion and discipline.
Flame cleanses and preserves the righteous.
Magic is dangerous when left unchecked by divine order.
</div>
<div>
  
**Legacy:**
Led the faithful into conflict with the Brotherhood of Nymeris. A crusade he inspired ended in the mysterious Silence beneath the Necropolis, and his prophet, Saint Velariun, was never seen again.
</div>
</details>

# Nakir’el 
<details open><summary>Click to expand/collapse Nakir'el</summary>

**The Moon God**

  <div>
    
**Title:** The Sleepless One, Shadowbinder, Keeper of Secrets
**Domains:** Darkness, lies, dreams, vengeance
**Symbols:** a dark crescent, a black hole, owls
**Sacred Color:** Silver & Indigo
**Devotees:** The Nakirathi wear indigo and moon-silver adornments and perform rites in moonlit ruins or sacrificial sites.
**Ritual:** At moonrise, they gather and chant to ward off illusions and invite wisdom from dreams.
</div>
<div>
  
Long ago, the Moon God and Sun Goddess danced in the sky in harmony and bliss. They adored the other's presence and they realised that one cannot be without the other. They were in love and the mortals worshipped them in all their glory. 
</div>
<div>
  
Though, the mortals favoured the Sun Goddess more who brought life in to their lands, growing crops and lighting the way to those who were lost. The Moon God was confused, why were the mortals not praising him? After thinking of a solution, he thought that perhaps borrowing some power from the Sun Goddess would help convince the mortals to praise him too. 
</div>
<div>
  
To steal her power, he caused an eclipse. Mortals grew scared at the lack of sunlight and the Sun Goddess was angered that her light was hidden. She and the mortals would not allow this and banished the Moon God, making it so that they were never seen together in the sky any more. 
</div>
<div>
  
In a fit of rage and jealousy, the Moon God who now hated the mortals that banished him, decided to create night creatures - Vampires, to be humanity's worst enemy. 
</div>
</details>

<details open><summary>Click to expand/collapse Pingus & Bwandwith'th</summary>

<div>
Twins who find mischievous joy in the acceleration and slowing of time, the addition and removal of languages to beings whomst had never spoken them before. 
</div>
<div>
Pranksters who cackle as their unwilling followers succumb to sudden fits of sleep, teleportation and all around weirdness as never seen before
</div>
<div>
Born from a stutter in the cosmos, these twins find joy in every inconvenience. 
</div>
<div>
Warriors of Pingus may find their sword swinging through their foe, their spells launching backwards, or the horses teleporting far from home
</div>
<div>
Scholars of Bwandwith’th often find their speech distorted, their words spliced in half, and worst of all the effect of walking into the room and forgetting what they were doing.

## The Green Mother
<details open><summary>Click to expand/collapse The Green Mother</summary>
  
**Goddess of the Natural**

<div>
Worshiped primarily by druids, fey-touched, and witches of the green hand path. The Green Mother is a goddess whose worship transcends borders and nationalities. Shrines are found on the edge of farms, within dense forests, and along mountain paths. All who venture frequently into the cradle of the natural world leave her offerings for a safe journey.
</div>
<div>
Iconography: She is depicted as a large green woman, often with arms full of flowers or vegetables. The Green Mother is a goddess of peace and healing, often holding potions or salves when shown in statues and portraits.
</div>
<div>
Names: The Green Mother
    The Mother
    The Green Lady
    The Lady Mother
  </div>
