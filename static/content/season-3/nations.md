# Vardoran, the Contested Heartland
*Colony of Nosterra, Abandoned 1-AF*

## Historical Overview
Vardoran is an ancient community that has evolved many times over the course of its long history. It was once a settlement of Nosterra, settled around the same time as Ajania. When Nosterra fell in 1-AF, Vardoran was abandoned as a colony and never reabsorbed. It crumbled without support from the Empire. For nine hundred years, nothing much happened. Just the gradual decline of civilization.

Before the Drac War, the Militia was much smaller and less well trained. They really had to shape up to deal with him. Brighthaven would prefer not to have such a largely armed and well trained population, but the Militia is older than Brighthaven. It is a long holdover from Nosterra that simply never left.

The oldest building in Vardoran is the Colosseum. It predates everything else still standing.

Vardoran had some history and culture before the Luminance and Transcendum ever arrived. They were never seafaring. They have always been insular.

Much of the world did not know Vardoran even existed until Dracula's rise in 1263-AF. It is under considerable scrutiny now, as some blame the region for magic's existence.

## Cultural Overview
The Church of Luminance holds dominant influence over Vardoran. After the war with Dracula, Luminance and the Militia became close allies. The Transcendum has a temporary truce with Luminance because they worked together during the war. This does not stop them from arming and enforcing their borders.

Old Vardoranian traditions persist. Harvest festivals, fertility festivals, and winter festivals are still celebrated.

Different factions of the land are always at each other's throats. The people of Vardoran really only consider Dunley and Silverlight to be part of the country.

The Noctum are much more cultlike than organized. They want to restore Dracula to his full power. They are not a competent military. They kidnap and indoctrinate citizens, turning those who drink the kool-aid into vampires and making them feed on their former cellmates. Some Noctum cultists are undercover in other societies. Qalidran in particular is wary of them. Noctum has no real leadership beyond a general deference to the person who turned you, like a pyramid scheme.

## Racial and Magical Overview
The average Vardoranian's stance on magic, other races, and anything unusual is simple. Magic is scary and bad. Mytts are scary. Werewolves are scary. Vampires are scary. Even Talam are scary. The prevailing attitude is: just let me farm.

## Important Allies and Enemies
Vardoranians are distrustful of outsiders for good reason. Novosvet is their ostensible largest ally, as it considers them a protectorate. Norus is also an important benefactor, but most people would rather not have both groups in the land fighting over their country.

Lakkrah is bad.

The books no longer hold good information. Many are word salad that seem cognizant and coherent only when paying minimal attention. Maja has been studying here since before the blood rain. The Cult of the Damned are not only worshippers of undeath. They are attempting to revive the greatest empire to ever grace the planet. Kriig, Gaius, and Bane are all resurrected Nosterrans with varying degrees of memory. Bane has the least mental capacity beyond his mission to murder. Kriig retains his tactical mind and goals. Gaius retains the most of his own personhood. The more freedom the undead has, the less of themselves they retain. Most undead are not Nosterrans, but Vardoranians from the past millennium.

### Silverlight's Mines
These were originally a cave system that people hid in to avoid the harpies. Silver was found here by the Nosterrans, so it has been catalogued for centuries. It was not worth extracting until more recent years. This is Dracula's fault.

# Aetheria, the Land of the Air and Stars
*Founded in 1268-AF*

## Historical Overview
Aetheria. The Land of the Air and Stars. It is a collection of three large islands and eight small ones. It is said that Aetheria is a mountain range that rose from the sea with the emergence of magic. Their shores are sharp, and their peaks high above the clouds. The land is difficult to navigate, and thus innately safe from outsiders.

Following the emergence of magic in 1268-AF, Talam gathered around the three islands of Aetheria: Solaria, Twilaria, and Lunaria, and formally founded their nation. Aetheria rests atop a multi-pronged magical hotspot. A massive wellspring of natural magics, it acts as a bastion against the magical realm, constantly pushing to balance the surge of magic that occurred. It has made magic and vegetation plentiful, despite the harsh climate of the tall mountains.

Aetherians are dedicated to the study of magic. The Talam wish to gather more of their people, and to understand the strange world in which they now find themselves. The study of magic is vital, and the passion of all Aetherians.

Their nation is a young one, ruled over by the founding Talam who found the islands and declared them home. Aetherians refer to this collective as The Eleven. They serve as a judicial and logistical ruler, deciding where paths will be built and bridges will be implemented. The Eleven are a mix of the three Circles, not favoring one over either of the others.

The Eleven are spiritual leaders, trusted elders, and beloved members of their communities. They are listened to when they speak, venerated by their peers for finding the beautiful mountains that Aetherians call home. All eleven of them are Talam.

## Cultural Overview
Each island is centralized around the Three Circles: Talaghria on Solaria, Talagaela on Twilaria, and Talafaesga on Lunaria. However, travel between the three is encouraged and very common. The mountains are very close together, so much so that the three islands can be walked between during low tide.

Large bridges connect the mountains, each made from cables of metal and durable planks of wood. Pillars have been built from the sea to act as foundations for the bridges. They are a modern architectural marvel, able to withstand high winds and the spray of the sea.

Known for its exports of rare silks, gems, jewelry, and things not of this realm, Aetheria is a land where the impossible is made real, a place of indescribable beauty and grace, of islands that pierce the sky, wreathed in mist and clouds. Its people find joy in the elegant simplicity of a life seeped in magic.

Talam culture is synonymous with Aetherian culture. They have a healthy respect for magic and the natural world, living side by side with nature. Balance between the living and the natural world is a core tenet of their beliefs. As is worship of the Sun.

Invention that relies on burning machines and bottled lightning is not prized, it is forbidden. For to challenge the power of magic's rule is to challenge the way of the world itself. The balance and natural order is coveted. Those who step outside the lines are swiftly banished from Aetheria's peaks.

Aetherians eat from carefully maintained terrace farms and the generous fresh water springs the mountains provide. It is a place of beauty: abundant waterfalls, and curious collections of floating pebbles that circle the highest parts of the mountains. Some Aetherians believe that, should they fully grasp the depths of magic, someday they, too, shall fly.

## Racial and Magical Overview
While roughly 95% of the population is Talam, there are others, mostly people with magic, amongst their number. All who share their beliefs or a healthy willingness to learn, are welcome in Aetheria.

Magic and the natural world are synonymous and equally valued. The Talam believe in the beauty of the natural world, and the importance of its balance.

## Important Allies and Enemies
The nation of Aetheria is young, barely created and not yet present on the world stage.

They have a healthy distaste for nations like Norus that rely on machines.

All Aetherians are wary of outsiders, unless those outsiders are visibly Talam.

## Important and Notable Landmarks/Places
### Solaria, the Isle of the Sun
The first and tallest peak, Solaria rests to the east and basks in the suns' rays far longer than the other two islands. They grow the bulk of the food here, as Solaria's faces are the softest of the three mountains, with the most room for terrace farms and people's homes.

People live in small mountaintop villages, and around their terrace farms. The main port is at the mountain's base, along with several fishing villages and trade hubs.

### Twilaria, the Isle of Twilight
Twilaria is the southernmost island, and rests firmly in Solaria's shadow. As a result, the island only gets light in the latter half of the day. Twilaria is their center of industry, where their tailors raise domesticated silk worms and their artisans build their bridges.

The mountaintop is flat, and home to Aetheria's only forest, the Twilight Grove. Maintaining the health of the grove is considered both sacred and vital to the Talam who tend to it. Some live in tree houses, while others permanently camp on the forest floor.

### Lunaria, the Isle of the Moon
Sequestered between the other two islands, Lunaria sees precious little daylight, only glancing at the sun when it is at its highest. There is very little natural vegetation on Lunaria, but it has complex cave systems that are rich in gems and precious metals.

Residents of Lunaria dwell primarily in the caves and homes built into the mountainside. It is the steepest of the three islands, and due to the lack of light, is considered the most dangerous despite being the shortest.

## A Random Fun Fact
Aetherians believe that as they bolster their magic the mountains will grow taller and thus closer to the sacred Sun.

# Ajania, the Silver Kingdom
*Founded in ???-AF*

## Historical Overview
Two hundred years before the fall of [joebobempire], Ajania's capital was founded as a distant silver mining colony for [JoeBob Empire]. A legion of [Insert appropriate social class/warrior type here] settled the Gallant Mountains to secure valuable silver, copper, and lead for the Empire. From early years, plumes of smoke billowed up from the valleys and poisoned the clouds. Expended quarries and open-pit mines flooded into poisoned lakes. Workers collapsed after inhaling poisonous fumes from deep within the caves. The people of Ajania learned and adapted. They researched the causes of these disasters and made changes to prevent the worst impacts of their extraction. They developed treatments and medicines for the poisons their country was founded to work with, and they used their enormous stockpiles of silver to leverage surrounding nations to trade them necessary resources.

Ajania quickly grew to become one of the world's wealthiest and best defended countries. It persisted after the fall of [joebobempire] and claims to still be the rightful heir-state to that empire. For centuries, Ajania ruled the region as the de-facto power, influencing other countries around it.

Eventually though, seven hundred years ago, silver lost favor as the currency for trade. Gold replaced it and Ajania's influence quickly declined. Lead and copper became more valuable exports and silver was no longer nearly as valuable. During this era, silver became an excess material that was used wherever it could find use. Usually it was simply used as part of construction. Ore-bearing stone was cut into bricks as much as any other masonry. Many of these buildings still stand today.

Three hundred years ago, silver's value again skyrocketed. The rise of the first Vampires and the introduction of magic into the world led to a practical and essential use for silver: the protection of humanity. With an increase of leisure that came with this wealth, Ajanians turned to expressions of their creativity and redoublings into their health. Doctors came from around the world to study in Ajanian hospitals. Skilled stone carvers received patronage to carve great sculptures into the peaks of the mountains surrounding the capital city, which were long since depleted of ore. In these past centuries, Ajania's importance and influence has turned it into a cultural and economic hub not just for the region, but for the world.

Now, with magic and monsters redoubling their effect on the world, Ajania finds itself a nation wealthy beyond words. Its people would be free to celebrate and live leisurely lives, if not for the recent troubling developments. Child and infant mortality and illness are on the rise. Inexplicably, some 15% of newborns in the country are facing severe illness. Those who survive tend to be sickly into their early childhood. Not enough time has passed yet to know for certain if they can eventually recover. If the cause of this illness is not found and solved, Ajania, a kingdom that stood for over a millennia, will collapse within a few short generations, and with it, the world's supply of silver.

# Alinar
*Founded in ???-AF*

## Historical Overview
Alinar is a prosperous land, full of fresh ideas and differing ideologies. A meritocracy where the fruits of one's labour speak loudest. Initially, the country was bound by limited fertile grounds but found its population booming as new methods to tame the surrounding territory were put into practice, quickly growing the country into the thriving state it is today.

Alinar's rapid growth and resilience was attributed to never being wholly reliant on any single solution. Their general open-mindedness has seen the country survive famine, drought, and war. Led by a triumvirate of elected leaders of their three leading philosophies; they are all kept in check by the Trancendus, Magiria, and the Agraria.

## Cultural Overview
The Trancendus believe that the way forward is through the wonders of technology, 'No reward without risk.' These are the boundary pushers, the chance-takers. They are the pioneers that find their truth in challenging the unknown. They do not fear failure.

The Magiria Collegiate are the wielders of the arcane in Alinar. They are the dreamers and work their miracles through magical prowess. 'The world and its bounties are vast, all that one can imagine can be attained.' They are the speculators, the generational planners. They look outwards for their inspiration and take what they've for their own people. Always have a back-up plan.

The Agraria are the traditionalists. They are not innovators like the other two, but instead far more practical in their approach. 'Cutting corners leads to cut fingers.' They are the robust, the brave, and perhaps the most thoughtful.

## Racial and Magical Overview
Generally affable thanks to their multicultural identity.

Magic is largely relegated to the Collegiate, where its use is almost an everyday occurrence. It's not considered strange to find magic in use elsewhere in Alinar, but it's usually a collaboration with the Magiria.

# Frankleburg, the Land of a Thousand Queens
*Founded in 701-AF*

## Historical Overview
They say that there are one thousand queens in Frankleburg--- each with her own small queendom and culture. Roughly a thousand years ago the queendoms united to fight off their northern neighbor's aggressions. Initially, the treaties were for the sake of defeating the larger enemy to the north, (INSERT COUNTRY NAME HERE). But, even after the war, the union of Frankleburgian queendoms made them prosper. They were stronger together. Richer. Safer.

The original 1,000 queens signed the Grand Traité; also known as the Golden Treaty, and formally founded Frankleburg. One nation, united for trade, prosperity, and defense.

Today, the Queens of Frankleburg gather at the Ministry of Queens, where they vote on domestic and foreign affairs. While most Queens have their title via birth, in recent years a handful of them have been elected to the position by the peoples of their queendoms.

Perhaps due to its size, since its foundation, Frankleburg has been directly involved in very few wars. It is a large, and powerful nation. Rich in steel and gold. Frankleburg not only controls the second-largest navy in the world, but also one of the largest land armies.

## Cultural Overview
Frankleburgians pride themselves on being the great Creuset-- melting pot-- with a thousand different little cultures and peoples. Frankleburgians take great pride not only in their national identity, but also in their smaller, ancestral lineages.

""To be different is to be strong"" is a common motto in Frankleburg. Differences are celebrated. Those who shun others for those differences are, in turned, shunned. There are numerous legends about warm Frankleburgian hospitality. Being welcoming to all is an important point of their culture.

Frankleburg has a capitalistic economic structure, and is a constitutional monarchy.

It has no single religion, and is aggressive in maintaining a separation of church and state. Many of the western queendoms are of the Luminance faith, while their coastal regions worship The Fish. Many queendoms have niche, region-specific religious practices that are unique to their micro-culture.

## Racial and Magical Overview
With Frankleburg's aggressive focus on growth, prosperity, and the strength in differences, Franklebrug is one of the few countries who has taken the emergence of magic in stride. There is a race boiling away just under the surface. Every scholar wants to be the first one to unravel the world's magical mysteries.

However, Franklebrug remains largely hostile to vampires-- certain queendoms outright banning their participation. It is illegal for vampires to kill humans in Frankleburg, and feeding may only happen with explicit consent.

# Hafjallheim
*Founded in ???-AF*

## Historical Overview
The history of Hafjallheim and her people have been passed down orally from times long before history remembers. These tales were protected by the people with great care through generations. Two hundred and twenty years ago much of this was lost. A great flood sent by the Storm Speaker, one of their gods, was endured to revitalize the land but took with it many artifacts and people who knew the tales of old.

This tragedy left the people with the hardship of piecing it back together through fragmented understanding. Tales tell during the restoration one of the new, long lived folk called Vampires showed up briefly and assisted greatly in the restoration. It is said their knowledge was so complete the people wondered if they had originally been from Hafjallheim. This person has now been immortalized in a legend whether it is true or not, as an explorer whose boat shows up out of the fog to a port which needs their assistance, and even they do not know where they will end up next.

The people of Hafjallheim believe their history to be this: An ancient group of nomadic people would roam the mountain range. These groups are known today as the Ashfolk. They roamed from peak to valley to river with their stout horses and shallow boats. These ancient familial houses were separate and would raid or trade with those around them including each other. One great historical hero, Hamundr the grand, is known to have conquered and united the houses under one banner, not against a great threat but in pursuit of greatness itself. In his time Hamundr took many wives to solidify his claim and now many families claim him as an ancestor. One of these wives was said to have been a daughter of the Frost Father. He gained her favor after slaying a grand serpent which has now been understood to be the raging sea itself. The Frost Father's daughter was thought to have predictions which brought early Hafjallheim to a grand era before it is said her and Hamundr set off in the very boat of which he tamed the sea to explore it together, never to return.

Another figure of myth is Fastulfr, the forge master of old who tamed the wolves of the forest, creating dogs with the sound of his striking hammer. So blessed was his hammer that Storm Speaker empowered it to bring forth a mountain from the earthen rock. Everyone says the mountain they are presently on is the mountain.

A third figure of myth is a child of the Frost Father born to a mortal, said to possess immense foresight. She gave six predictions. Whether any of these figures truly lived as told is a matter of faith.

## Cultural Overview
### Faith Within Hafjallheim
Most in Hafjallheim follow the Faith of Shifting Eons, the true cycle of existence and thought. They follow the duality of the Storm Speaker and the Frost Father. Both the Storm Speaker, or Hreggmathr as they are known in Hafjallheim, and Frost Father, or Hrimfathir as he is known in Hafjallheim, are seen as a forever cycle. Storm Speaker is seen as speaking into the sky often, whereas Frost Father only speaks when needed. They represent a cycle of action and inaction, activity and introspection. When the ice melts, waters swell, animals come out of hibernation, and productivity is free to happen. When the ice settles in again it is a time to review what one has done, learned, and how one interacts with the world. Storm Speaker is always changing while Frost Father is set in his ways.

In recent years Luminance has started creeping into the minds and ways of this nation. There is a duality between those who feel more drawn by the new faith and those who stick to the old faith.

### The Three Casts of Hafjallheim
Within Hafjallheim there are no differences in gender expectations, but instead three groups of people all viewed as necessary for their way of life. Other nations have come to call them casts, which the newer schools of thought have taken to using as well. These casts are based on the three cloak types found from the Ashfolk of old. The Hyrr who wear wildfire cloaks of old, the Frer who wear crystal ice cloaks of old, and the Farmathr who wear a more extravagant furred cloak, a king's mantle.

The Hyrr are warriors, hunters, defenders, berserkers. They are highly active and vigilant in their jobs, thus drawing the eye and favor of the Storm Speaker. They serve as both the hunters and the military of Hafjallheim. The Hyrr cast are known for sending the newly joined off to hunt or fetch something that does not exist, and many of this cast are met with some apprehension or fear. Getting old in this cast is a monumental feat.

The Frer are the craftsfolk, artisans, farmers, herders, and local traders of Hafjallheim. They are essential in that they feed the other casts, provide hospitality, and keep the houses. In times which call for it the Frer discuss and draw borders, negotiate peace, and make reparations. Their focus on things of the home and relations to people garner them attention from the Frost Father.

The Farmathr are travelers, explorers, heroes, and merchants. They go out with wares into the greater world to gather trade, experiences, and gain glory. As the Farmathr cast go out into the world they often are asked to buy specific things by those in their home towns, and are simply given the estimated coin or goods required to gain these items. It is the Farmathr's duty to learn of and from other cultures, policies, and governments to better improve Hafjallheim when they return home. They have often been referred to as the Kings of Hafjallheim, even though King in the context of Farmathr is less a ruler and more a leader of ventures.

Switching casts is not a common thing but it isn't unheard of. Switching too often however may come with a stigma of disloyalty and flakiness. Hyrr who have been injured often switch to Frer cast.

### The People's Culture
The people of Hafjallheim hold a duality in their culture which is rooted deep in their ancestral memory and faith of the Shifting Eons. They believe that life is filled with both trial and levity, and one must appreciate both to truly live. As descendants of the Ashfolk, the people of Hafjallheim hold old traditions in high regard, but because they are no longer nomadic those traditions have had to change alongside them. Coming of age ceremonies are based on each house or family called an Ayett. Each house has their own trial one must overcome to become considered a well adjusted adult. Some of these include finding a specific flower by the river, taming a wild horse, hunting one's first deer, hitting a single strike during a fight with the family's strongest or most skilled, or making one's first high value trade. The old tales say the Ashfolk crown artifacts were given to those who underwent their trials as a form of recognition in the tribes of old. This is still kept as a tradition to this day.

Weddings and betrothals are similar in that becoming betrothed involves offering a crafted weapon or tool to the intended fiance. When two are to be wed they must each perform the coming of age ceremony of their partner's house before the celebration and feast begins.

When one's time has been called for eternal rest by the cycle a funeral is held by either way of sky or marsh. Those who follow the old ways keep to sky burials, hoping for many corvids to show up as they will carry souls on their wings to where they must go. The newer style of funeral consists of laying one to rest in the muck of Stormryr, the great marsh. The deeper one is laid to rest, the more hope of being remembered for years to come.

Many festivals are held across the country but none are more impactful than the Festival of Colors and the Frost Feast. During the Festival of Colors everyone picks a dye to dip cloth or string to decorate a tree, as they ready themselves for the active season and send well wishing for the King's Cloak cast as they leave for their adventures. The Frost Feast is a festival where the majority of the country comes together in the gathering halls at the edge of the Skogwyrd to observe the first frost and the homecoming of the King's Cloaks.

Homes in Hafjallheim nowadays are built partly into the ground or the side of the mountains. The buildings placed the highest up the mountains are governmental and religious with one military building, while other military buildings are scattered through the edges. Generally these buildings are covered in dirt and have various plants found in the region growing atop them. Some still cling to old traditions of having movable homes made of canvas and furs with sleds made of shallow boats, though this is less common in modern times.

### The Economic Situation
Much of Hafjallheim's economy is based around trade routes first carved out both across land and sea in search of pigments and dyes. The people developed a keen eye for these dyes and other valuables over the decades, and have become known as fair traders. Since colors and pigments have captured the imaginations of the people, dyes have become their preferred currency, purple being the highest value. Coins are less valued, but there is an up and coming thought process that coins are used everywhere else and thus they must adapt to use of this currency. The compromise in recent years is painting parts of coins in the valued colors. Due to the paint scratching off easily they have been looking into ways to dye the soft metals. This has led to more trade with Transcendum dominated countries like Norus in hopes to use their technology for this purpose.

The majority of Hafjallheim consists of humans and has for a long time.

# The Hallowed Mountains

## Historical Overview

## Cultural Overview
There are two distinct groups that live within The Hallowed Mountains, those who live in the valleys, and those who live on the mountains.

The monks give these Hallowed Mountains their name. And although the mountains are considered impenetrable to most, the monks can take travelers through, at a steep cost.

The Hallowed Mountains are not a formal government at all. Most of the monasteries barely interact with one another, but these people have retained their independence through their dedication to thriving in such an inhospitable land.

With the rise of magic, many monks who have spent decades in careful introspection and honing of their bodies, minds, and souls, find that they have natural aptitudes towards magics of Light and Frost.

Nearly all the people of the mountains are human. Vampires rarely stay long in a region with so little prey, so little cover from the sun, and so many capable light-wielders. The monks drove off the incursions of Dracula when he rose to power, or rather, the lands themselves did. ## Cultural Overview
Qalidran is both the name of the nation and its only city. It sits at the center of the island, made up of four regions: the Inner Palace, the Terrace, the Gardens, and the Slums.

The distance between a vampire and the Progenitor informs two things: their political power in Qalidran and their might as a vampire. The closer one is to the Progenitor, the more powerful their magic and the strain of their vampirism.

*Please note that player vampires may not be closer than 4th generation. There are at least three vampires between you and the Progenitor.*

Qalidran relies on barter systems. They trade for tools, fabric, and gold. Blood is given to all who call it home on a regular basis. The higher your social standing, the purer the blood you are supplied. However, even those at the lowest rungs of the social ladder never go hungry. Access to blood is considered a right, one guaranteed by the laws of the land.

Honor and social standing is everything in Qalidran. Crossing the wrong vampire may mean death for you and your clan. It is a complex hierarchy of vampires, as difficult as it is dangerous to navigate.

All of Qalidran is one castle heart, which other clans have been allowed to build their castles within. The size sigils are given is directly related to their population, utility, and military prowess. Clans who lose the favor of the Descendants will feel their walls closing in, quite literally. Those foolish enough to rebel will simply find themselves without a roof come the following morning. To cross the Descendants is to court Death.

Vampires of Qalidran are split into five major groups: The Parlor, The Temple, The Harvest, The Navy, and The Guard. Each of these groups are beneath the control of one of the Progenitor's Five Descendants. Their faces and names are not known. They rarely leave the inner palace and never do so without heavy guard.

### The Parlor
The heart of Qalidran's political body. It is a tiered system, all serving the First Descendant. Every sigil may only have one of their members, usually their sigil leader, attend meetings. The Parlor meets within the Crimson Hall, a large room built in the outermost area of the Inner Palace, and is the only part of the Inner Palace that is accessible to those who do not live within it.

The High Table, which is located behind a screen, is made up of ten members, all of whom are believed to be second and third generation vampires of the Five Descendants. Their names and identities beyond their relationship to the Progenitor are shrouded in mystery. The First Descendant may veto or approve any matter that is brought to the Parlor without vote.

Beneath them is the Low Table, which is filled with primarily fourth and fifth generation vampires. Some vampires who are very wealthy, have large clans, or are uniquely powerful, may find themselves at the Low Table. The Low Table has 80 members. These members vote on matters that are presented to the Parlor. Should they tie, the High Table discusses the matter and makes the final decision.

Getting an invitation to the Low Table is a great honor. Clans engage in complicated political games to try and garner their seat. It is a source of betrayal, murder, and strife.

Beyond the Low Table is the Parlor's Congregation: a group of 120 people who are the heads of the rest of Qalidran's sigils. The Congregation is made up of vampires who lack power, are of small clans, or have little to no relation to the Progenitor. They do not get a vote, nor can they speak during Parlor gatherings, but they may bring written matters for the Parlor to discuss if they obtain the signatures of fifty clans. Members of the Congregation sit in long rows on bench seats below the Low Table.

There is a great deal of standing room around the outer ring of the room, between the Congregation seating and the walls. The public is able to watch Parlor proceedings, but they must remain silent or they will be punished.

Lesser criminals, those involved in robbery, fights, and the like, have their punishments decided by a jury composed of ten members of the Lower Table.

Major crimes, such as murder, unauthorized human contact, and terrorism, have their punishments decided by two members of the Upper Table.

### The Temple
The spiritual body of Qalidran is under the domain of the High Priestess, the Second Descendant. She leads all major religious rituals. She is one of the only Descendants who is seen by the people at large. However, she hides her face behind a mask and stands with three other women who share her hair color and build. It is impossible to determine who is the Second Descendant, and who is merely a decoy.

The Temple worships the Progenitor. They believe the first vampire to be the source of all the vampiric magics, and all of the gifts that they enjoy as a result. Human sacrifice and ritual blood letting are common practices.

The Temple claims that the Progenitor is currently in torpor, replenishing his magics after giving the gift of new magic to vampires in 324-PA.

### The Harvest
Members of the Harvest are the vampires who serve under the Third Descendant, and are those entrusted with the care of Qalidran's human residents. They ensure that humans are healthy and happy. After all, it is blood that thrums with life that is the most delicious.

The Harvesters are leaders in agriculture. They help their humans tend to their farms and livestock. They ensure that should one region face a difficult growing season, the other villages pick up their slack.

Many of the Harvest are also doctors and alchemists. Their primary duty is to ensure that the human population remains healthy. They respond quickly to any threat of disease or harm towards the humans. Members of the Harvest are the only vampires in Qalidran permitted into the human settlements, which are locked behind massive walls and fortified gates. It is a position that is only granted to those who have earned it.

The Harvest's other duty is to teach the humans within their settlements and find those with promise. Powerful mages or future brilliant scholars who may yet be worthy of the gift of vampirism. Only the truly excellent are offered a chance at unlife.

### The Navy
Under the control of the Fourth Descendant, the Navy maintains Qalidran's borders. They ride great ships that carry mist braziers. The Fourth Descendant rides upon Sunderfin, the great wyrm. It is a large, undead sea serpent. It is the primary reason for Qalidran's supremacy at sea. Sunderfin can sink ships in a single attack, and is the reason why the waters around Qalidran never see human boats merely passing through.

Qalidran's marines are the few with permission to ferry vampires between Qalidran and the mainland. Boats only make the journey four times a year, and always under heavy guard. They also man all of the outposts along Qalidran's outer walls.

If disaster were to strike one of the human settlements on Qalidran, it is the navy who would go ashore on the mainland to replace the lost blood supply by any means necessary.

### The Guard
Under the control of the Fifth Descendant, the Guard keeps the nation safe. They ensure that vampires follow the laws of the land, and keep any who are not related to the Descendants out of the Inner Palace.

All members of the Guard wear masks, referred to as Reds and Grays. Those with red masks reside in and around the inner palace. The Red Guard is made up entirely of those within three generations of the Progenitor.

Those from the fourth generation or farther out are members of the Gray Guard. Grays guard the large vampiric city that makes up the bulk of Qalidran, ensuring order and loyalty among the vampires who call the island home. Others are stationed at the outer walls, or protect the Parlor while it is in session. The unlucky few serve as guardians of The Fifth Hell, the prison that lies at the depths of the capital's sewers.

## Racial and Magical Overview
85% to 90% of the world's vampires reside in Qalidran. Their vampiric population measures somewhere between 8,500 and 9,000. Their humans have flourished over the years, creating large coastal villages and communities. The number of humans on the island of Qalidran fluctuates around 100,000.

Qalidran has had mixed reactions to the emergence of the other magical races. Some view them as an opportunity for study and growth, while others discard them as useless for their lack of the vampiric magics. For now, their walls remain closed, but interested, in those who are not vampires.

Werewolves have been banned from the island after an entire village was slaughtered by one during a blood moon. Following the incident, newly acquired humans from the mainland are quarantined through their first full moon to ensure that no werewolves breach their human populations.

Qalidran's main city and capital is a single structure created out of the world's largest castle heart. Blood magic is plentiful within its walls, and is considered to be the superior strain of magic by most of its residents. However, most vampires have been quick to embrace the gift of new magic at their fingertips.

Giving the gift of vampirism is a big deal in Qalidran. Clans must put in a request to the Parlor to be given a human to turn. That human is selected for it from the human settlements. Only the best and brightest are given the opportunity to become a vampire. It is never their choice, however, and they simply go where they are told. To ensure that clans remain a controllable size, sigils within Qalidran are never larger than fifty in number.

Qalidran has no allies, only enemies. They consider non-Qalidran ships kill on sight and will sink anyone they find wandering in their waters regardless of the ship's size.

## Important and Notable Landmarks/Places
### The Fifth Hell
The largest prison that lies at the heart of Qalidran's capital. Many criminals are entombed in the walls, never to see the moonlight ever again. Vampires thrown into the Fifth Hell are left to starve as part of their punishment. Some perish due to starvation, while others waste away in their cells until their sentence ends. 70% of those thrown into The Fifth Hell never return to the surface. It is a maze-like structure built beneath the Terrace region of the city. It is full of traps, foul magic, and deadly pits. Rescue attempts end in death.

### The Inner Palace
The home of the Progenitor and his Descendants. Those within three generations of the Progenitor all reside within the palace. If they leave it, they are accompanied by a great number of guards and always wear masks and veils. Only the Crimson Hall is accessible to the public. The rest is locked away behind tall walls and heavy gates.

### The Terrace
Where most of the 80 members of the Lower Table reside. The largest and most powerful clans have been given the rights to build their homes within the Terrace district. The closer one resides to the Inner Palace, the more secure their position within Qalidran is.

### The Garden
The bulk of the population lives in the Garden district, which is also the largest ring of the city. It is called as such because it is a collection of large homes that are separated by vegetation and trees. This is where the city grows vampiric plants, such as blood roses and thistle, as well as the lumber they need for construction. The large market squares, artisans, tailors, and blacksmiths are all set up within the Garden district. Everyone is free to wander here, and it is where 90% of the city's trade occurs.

### The Slums
The outer ring of the city is the poorest area. This is the place for vampires without clans or access to the island's castle magics. They live in small hovels built by hand. Most are new arrivals, and the vast majority of the slum's residents will be adopted into clans once they have proven their worth. The Slums are the smallest district, filling the gaps between the Garden and the city's exterior walls. Some choose to live there with no intent of joining a clan. They are safe from the sun, the elements, and humans who want to kill them. They collect their septurn's ration of blood and keep to themselves.

### Human Settlements
There are a dozen human settlements spread around Qalidran. One is in a walled section of the Terrace, while all of the others are outside of the city. Most of them are coastal, so their humans may fish for food, and also have space to grow the food they need to survive. Humans within the settlements rarely rebel. Their lives are worth more to their vampire keepers when the blood that flows through their veins is happy and healthy. They get food, healthcare, and are protected from external threats. To quell human rebellions, the elderly no longer have to give blood and may retire to die of a ripe old age. Humans between fifteen and sixty are expected to donate their blood once or twice a month. Their culture has twisted over the centuries, painting the outside world as something harsh and horrible. They believe that it is safest within their walls. The last human rebellion was in 213-PA. There have been none since. Humans who excel in might, magic, or smarts may be selected to become a vampire. After they have done so, they are forbidden from returning to their hometowns for the rest of their very long lives. They are also ineligible to join The Harvest for at least a century.

## A Random Fun Fact
Qalidran is where most vampiric lingo originated, sigils, castle hearts, turn, tick, and more!

# The Sul'uun Empire
*Founded in 112-AF*

## Historical Overview
Sul'uun has a long, complicated history. A unification of tribes in 12-AF founded their kingdom in the heart of the forest of Uln. They enjoyed rapid expansion, plentiful harvests, and abundant rivers. In just a single century the Kingdom of Sul'uun became the largest country in the world.

At their onset, they worshiped a god of the sun. It was he who ensured their harvests were plentiful and their shores safe. It was to him that they pledged their loyalties. People of Sul'uun refer to him as the Great Sun, or the Holy Sun.

Their thirteenth king, and first emperor, was their undoing. Their greedy king turned his eyes beyond their borders, to the kingdom to their north, Lythe. Where Sul'uun worshiped the great and glorious Sun, Lythe worshiped the quiet splendor of the Moon. He fought long and hard to take over Lythe, but it was to no avail. He could not conquer their highland strongholds.

After years of war, the king of Lythe came to the thirteenth king with an offering. Join with his daughter in marriage, and lead both kingdoms together as Emperor and Empress of a new nation. He had but one condition. This newly founded empire was to worship the Moon. Not the Sun.

The greedy king agreed. He married the daughter of the moon and cast aside worship of the Sun. Sul'uun doubled in size, but their growth came at a cost that none were prepared to pay. The Empire of Sul'uun was founded in 334-AF, the first empire on their continent.

The Sun could not forgive their betrayal. Ever since that day, the Sun's rays have scorched the earth of Sul'uun. It is a land where rain never falls. The once plentiful forests are naught but a barren desert. Sand, red and burning, stretches as far as the eyes can see. Nothing grows in Sul'uun. The soil is unfit for life.

Now, the people of Sul'uun hide in their mountains to the north or the large, complex tunnels beneath the deadly sands. They beg the Sun to spare them, offering great tithes for mercy and forgiveness. The Sun does not listen.

## Cultural Overview
Sul'uun is a nation focused on survival. While there is some desert vegetation they can eat, they rely largely on hunting wyrms, large sand-dwelling beasts that dwell within the earth, for food. They drink the creature's blood and viscera instead of water. They eat its guts and fashion its hard shells into tools.

Sul'uun imports large amounts of dirt and water to grow what food they can. Food is grown in large, hanging baskets. Should those baskets ever touch the ground the soil becomes corrupted by the Sun God's Curse and turns into red sand. Baskets of soil are carried miles across the desert, into the subterranean cities or mountain settlements. To drop vital soil and tarnish it is a crime punishable by death.

Sul'uunians have adopted several features of the great wyrms that they are forced to consume. Many have red or yellow eyes, and their skin has taken on a reddish or pink hue. They are a culture that believes in punishment, hard work, and a hearty heaping of a fear for the divine.

"It is by the Mercy of the Moon that we survive. It is in the Curse of the Sun that we burn."

The Empire of Sul'uun is unique in its worship, as they have a dual faith serving both the Sun and the Moon, the faith of Luntoll. There are ceremonies performed every day and every night to assuage the Sun's ire, and try to stay in the Moon's good graces. Common rituals are nightly prayer, group meditations, and collective dance.

The people of Sul'uun believe that the union of Lythe and Sul'uun represented a marriage between the Sun and the Moon. The Sun is referred to with masculine pronouns, while the Moon is distinctly feminine. Weddings are held at sunset or sunrise, when the divine couple shares the sky. Weddings held during the day or night are considered to be doomed at their onset.

To be caught worshipping another deity within Sul'uun is a crime. They believe that the Sun would take it as an insult, and heretics are executed immediately to ensure that the Sun does not punish their neighbors for their foolish transgressions.

## Racial and Magical Overview
Mages able to assist in the production of food are elevated to nobility. Druidic and elemental mages are vital to the empire's continued survival. Sul'uun pays hefty coin to purchase mages from neighboring nations.

Vampires are exceptionally rare due to the unforgiving nature of the Sun. There are no trees to hide in, and scarce caves. While the cities are underground in large, man-made caverns, they do not take kindly to vampiric guests and would kill them upon discovery. A vampire would have to be exceptionally resourceful and skilled and more than a little lucky to traverse the sands alive.

## Important and Notable Landmarks/Places
### The Sul'uun Desert
The desert covers 95% of Sul'uun's territory. It is a harsh, unforgiving environment. The bugs are large, the days are scorching, and monsters prowl the sands for food at night. The sands are always shifting, with large mounds that are in a perpetual state of change. The sand is red. The color shifts to a more vibrant crimson the deeper one goes. The rocks beneath the ground are the same scarlet. Sul'uunians believe that all of Sul'uun is red, all the way down to the earth's heart. Luckily, Sul'uun is surrounded by mountains on all sides, so the desert has not spread beyond the peaks into the rest of the continent.

### Tolra, the Capital
The capital of Sul'uun is located in the heart of the desert. The palace of the first emperor's tallest spires still pierce the sand that has buried the rest of the city. They serve as a beacon to travelers lost in the desert, as they are tall enough to be seen from several leagues away. As the city sank, so too did its people. Tolra rebuilt beneath the sands. It is a huge collection of caves and caverns, some natural and others carved by hand. People live in small houses carved into the walls, separated by sheer staircases and precarious paths. The hanging gardens feed the people, watered with freshwater that they pull from the depths of the earth. In contrast to the bitter desert winds, the city is almost always cool. Sul'uunians wear heavy carapace from the wyrms and thick, layered blankets to stay warm. The blankets are dyed in elaborate stripes of red, gold, and orange. The thin outer layer is worn while on the surface. The further down you go, the more layers you add.

### The Wana Crater
The Wana Lake was once the largest freshwater lake on the continent. It was the Kingdom of Sul'uun's main source of water and fish. After the Curse of the Sun settled into the soil, the Wana Lake's water level fell and fell, until only a cracked crater remained. The Wana Crater is a stark reminder of the prosperity that they lost. Once a year, during the new year's ceremonies, faithful of Luntoll leave flowers, jewels, and other offerings at the crater's heart in the hopes that the new year will bring forgiveness and a return to the splendor they once knew.

## A Random Fun Fact
Luntoll faithful execute heretics with sand burials, burying them alive in deep pits of red sand.
