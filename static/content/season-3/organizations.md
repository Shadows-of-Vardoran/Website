The Church of Luminance is one of the oldest religious institutions in the world. Long before magic existed, the Church was already there, worshipping the sun and the light it cast across the world, preaching that darkness was a corruption to be driven back and that Luminance, their deity, stood eternal against it. For centuries it was simply faith. Prolific, widespread faith, the kind that finds particular purchase when monsters are hunting people in the dark, but faith nonetheless.

Then Dracula arrived, and everything the Church had ever preached suddenly had teeth.

When light magic manifested in the hands of the Church's faithful, it was treated as divine confirmation. Luminance had answered. Their greatest warrior, a figure known as Solarus, led the Church's forces against Dracula's campaign and ultimately delivered the killing blow at a site now known as Dracula's Demise. The Church emerged from the war as the most powerful magical institution in the world, the only organization outside of vampiric kind to wield magic at all.

It didn't last.

As magic emerged across the world it became rapidly apparent that the Church's power was not divine, singular, or exclusive. People who had never set foot in a chapel were casting spells, and the monopoly the Church had built its post-war authority on was slipping through their fingers before the dust had even settled. An institution that had just finished a holy war was suddenly faced with a world that no longer needed to come to them for magic.

They remain militant, deeply organized, and present in Vardoran. What they are looking for here, and what they are willing to do to find it, remains their own business.

They still hate vampires.

The Archivum is a coalition of scholars, researchers, and practitioners drawn together from across the world under a single unifying principle: that understanding magic is more important than controlling it.

They are politically neutral by design. The Archivum takes no side in the wars between nations, the feuds between clans, or the long and bloody history between vampires and the Vampire Hunters' Guild. These conflicts are noise to them. Their focus is magic itself, where it comes from, how it works, and what it means for a world that had none of it five years ago.

They are not an institution of teachers. They do not have answers to distribute. The Archivum is a community of people who are all, in one way or another, figuring this out together. The Archivum believes the full picture of magic cannot be assembled from any one perspective, and so all perspectives are welcome.

In Vardoran, the Archivum has established a presence following their scholar's discovery that this land sits at the origin point of magic's spread across the world. They are here to investigate, and they intend to be here for as long as that investigation takes. They will empower expeditions, encourage research, and extend an open hand to anyone willing to work alongside them.

Joining them is a choice, not a requirement. The Archivum asks only curiosity in return.

Transcendum is a secretive organization of mages and scholars who seek to transcend the limitations of the mortal form. Through a combination of blood magic, alchemy, and forbidden rituals, they pursue immortality, godhood, and knowledge that mortals were never meant to possess. Their influence is subtle, their members carefully hidden, and their goals inscrutable to outsiders.

Noctum is a network of vampires, rogues, and outcasts who operate in the shadows of Vardoran. They control the flow of information, run the black markets, and broker deals that the legitimate powers cannot acknowledge. Noctum is not a single organization but a loose affiliation of cells, each operating independently under a shared code of silence and mutual benefit.

<!-- section:vampire-hunters-guild -->

The Vampire Hunters' Guild is the oldest and most widely recognized organization in the world dedicated to the protection of humanity from supernatural threats.

Founded just under three hundred years ago in the wake of the vampiric scourge, the Guild was born from necessity. No single nation could stand against vampires alone. What was needed was cooperation, shared knowledge, and weapons that actually worked. The Guild provided all three. They forged silver into blades and bolts, catalogued vampiric weaknesses, and trained generations of hunters who slowly, painstakingly, pushed the vampires back. By the time Dracula arrived, the Guild had already spent centuries earning its reputation in blood.

That reputation has not made the intervening years easy. The advent of magic upended everything the Guild thought it understood about the threats it was built to face. Vampires remain their oldest enemy, but the spread of werewolves across human nations has stretched the Guild thin, and the appearance of entirely new kinds of beings has forced an organization built on accumulated knowledge to confront how much it does not yet know.

The Guild maintains a presence in Vardoran. Whether that presence is investigative, protective, or something more pointed depends entirely on who you ask.
