# RPO Tickets
<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

RPO tickets are your gateway to uncovering secret lore, exploring new abilities, and interacting with the plot of Vardoran in meaningful and unique ways.

An RPO ticket is usually written as a short narrative exchange between your character and the world. You describe your character’s actions, experiments, rituals, or investigations. An admin responds as a narrator, reflecting how the world reacts to what you attempt.

Not every RPO ticket, experiment, or ritual will result in something tangible or in new knowledge. Sometimes the lack of new information is still progress. It can rule out a method, refine understanding, or push your character to adapt and try something new. Science, magic, and rituals are not perfect.

This season, we want to encourage players to tell us more about what their characters are doing and why, instead of relying on the team to define every possible outcome.

</details>

## How RPO tickets Work

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

- RPO tickets are written from your character’s perspective.
- You describe what your character is doing, attempting, or experimenting with.
- The admin team responds as the world or as a narrative force.
- Outcomes are influenced by effort, logic, lore, and prior actions.
- Results may be success, partial success, failure, or new complications.

</details>


## General Expectations

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

- Come with a clear idea of what your character is attempting to discover, create, or understand.
- Identify something in the world you want to interact with beyond normal gameplay.  
  Examples include radiant fibers, gold ore, coal, perfect gems, cursed weapons, or unexplained phenomena.
- Know your goal and have a rough plan for how your character intends to pursue it.
- Include relevant details such as rituals attempted, locations visited, or groundwork already laid.
- Keep goals RP focused. RPO tickets should support your character’s story and motivations, not exist solely for rewards or power.

</details>


## What’s Changing This Season

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

In prior seasons, the team often helped guide players toward outcomes, especially when tickets were very light on detail. Some players provided extensive plans, while others provided very little and expected the rest to be filled in.

Going forward, RPO tickets are meant to be a collaborative creative space where you lead with intent and action, and the team responds with how the world reacts. This means expectations for what you bring into a ticket have increased slightly.

Before submitting an RPO, take some time to consider:
- What your character wants
- Why they want it
- How they plan to pursue it
- What actions they have already taken

Plans do not need to be perfect. Failed experiments, incorrect assumptions, and setbacks are all valid and often lead to better story.

</details>

## Examples of RPO Goals

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

- A scholar researching why a cursed weapon behaves differently under moonlight.
- An alchemist testing the stability of radiant fibers through controlled experiments.
- A mage attempting a risky ritual based on incomplete or fragmented lore.
- A character revisiting a past failure with a new theory or approach.

</details>

## Ability Commands

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

You can assign spells to your unarmed Q and E slots using the commands below.

Spells are copied from your current primary and secondary spell slots, so make sure those are set before running the command.

### Set Abilities
- `.sov ability set q` or `.sov ability set primary`  
  Copies the spell from your first spell slot (R) to Weapon Slot 1 (Q).

- `.sov ability set e` or `.sov ability set secondary`  
  Copies the spell from your second spell slot (C) to Weapon Slot 2 (E).

### Clear Abilities
- `.sov ability clear q` or `.sov ability clear primary`  
  Clears the spell assigned to Weapon Slot 1 (Q).

- `.sov ability clear e` or `.sov ability clear secondary`  
  Clears the spell assigned to Weapon Slot 2 (E).

</details>

# How to Whitelist Your Character

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>
This guide is here to help you understand what information matters most in a whitelist application, how to present it clearly, and what generally fits within the setting.

Think of this less as a checklist and more as guidance on how to communicate who your character is, what drives them, and why they belong in Vardoran.

A strong whitelist focuses on the parts of your character that actively shape how they think, act, and interact right now. Not every detail of their life story needs to be included. What matters most are the forces pushing them forward.
</details>

---

## What to Focus On

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

**Knowledge, Skills, and Interests**
- Explain any magical, alchemical, technological, or scientific interests your character has pursued.
- These should be framed as studies, experiments, research interests, partial successes, or failures.
- Fully finished, mass-producible, or easily deployable inventions are not appropriate at character start.
- If your character has seen or assisted with advanced work, that does not mean they fully understand or can recreate it.

**Level of Expertise**
- Be realistic and clear about how skilled your character actually is.
- If they worked in a controlled institution such as a Nexarcan laboratory, explain what they realistically had access to.
- Most people only ever understand a small piece of a much larger system.

**Formative Life Events**
- Highlight pivotal moments that shaped who your character is today.
- This can include trauma, major failures, personal losses, or defining successes.
- These moments should explain how your character reacts to stress, conflict, or opportunity.

**Personality**
- Describe traits that will actively show up in roleplay.
- Examples include ambition, loyalty, pride, insecurity, anger, curiosity, compassion, or stubbornness.
- Focus on traits that create interaction, not just descriptors.

**Why Vardoran**
- Explain why your character came to Vardoran.
- What pushed them away from their previous life?
- What pulled them here?
- What do they want, and what keeps them moving forward?

**Ambitions and Goals**
- If your character has large goals, especially magical, scientific, or world-altering ones, explain how they plan to pursue them.
- The larger the ambition, the more detail we need to understand the path you intend to take.
</details>

---

## What to Avoid

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

**Family and Backstory**
- Avoid extensive family trees, childhood anecdotes, or romantic histories unless they directly affect your character’s current motivations.
- Distant relatives, old crushes, or minor childhood events rarely matter for approval.
- You are welcome to keep extra lore for personal use, but it is not required for whitelisting.

- Do not write direct connections to existing in-game characters such as Dracula or other VBloods.
- Your character should stand on their own, not be pre-attached to major canon figures.

**Realistic Powerlevel**
- Do not write your character as all-powerful, invincible, or universally exceptional.
- Geniuses, master warriors, or flawless problem-solvers must be supported by both setting logic and player capability.
- If you are still learning the game mechanically, consider starting your character earlier in their journey.
- Growth through roleplay is always more satisfying than starting at the peak.

**Hard Stops**
- Do not copy characters wholesale.
- Inspiration is encouraged, but take a single idea or theme and build something original around it.

**Suggested Guidelines**
- Avoid trying to make your character untouchable.
- Weaknesses, flaws, vices, and emotional soft spots create stronger roleplay.
- Pride, fear, addiction, guilt, or misplaced loyalty give others ways to meaningfully engage with your character.
</details>

---

## Technology and Advancement

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

- Do not assume technology progressed the same way it did in the real world.
- Magic fundamentally altered scientific and technological development in this setting.
- Two hundred years in Vardoran does not equal two hundred years of real-world advancement.

- Technological stagnation, regression, or divergence is expected.
- If you want advanced creations, consider how magic supplements or replaces technology instead of copying real-world designs.

- Anything truly advanced must be earned through roleplay.
- Progress should involve effort, setbacks, and staff oversight.
- Starting with partial knowledge and growing over time is the intended experience.
</details>

---

## Final Notes

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

This guide is not meant to limit creativity. It exists to keep the setting grounded, collaborative, and fun for everyone.

If you are ever unsure whether something fits, ask. We are always happy to help you shape an idea into something that works within the world. Opening an RPO ticket is always a good place to start.
</details>

# Creating a Discord Character Profile

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

Character profiles are an optional tool to help other players and staff quickly understand who your character is at a glance. They are not required, and there is no expectation that every field be filled out.

You may include as much or as little information as you like. Some players prefer a minimal profile with only a name and appearance, while others enjoy writing something more detailed. Both approaches are valid.

This profile is meant to support roleplay, not replace it.
</details>


## How to Use This Template

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

- You do not need to fill in every section.
- Skip anything that does not apply to your character.
- You may rename, remove, or add sections to better fit your character.
- This profile can evolve over time as your character grows through roleplay.

Think of this as a living document, not a form to complete.
</details>



## Core Character Information

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

These fields help identify your character and how they are generally known.

- **Name**
- **Titles**  
  Formal titles, honors, or reputations your character holds.
- **Alias**  
  Nicknames, false names, or alternate identities.
- **Significant Other** (if applicable)
- **House**
- **Former House** (returning characters only)
- **Position**  
  Rank, role, or standing within an organization or faction.
- **Sigil**  
  Heraldry or symbolic markings, if any.
</details>



## Identity and Physical Description

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

Use as much or as little detail as you wish. This section exists to help others visualize your character.

- **Gender**
- **Pronouns**
- **Height**
- **Age Appearance**  
  How old your character appears to be.
- **Actual Age**
- **Eye Color**
- **Hair Color**
- **Skin Color**

**Physical Appearance**  
A brief description of your character’s build, posture, notable scars, clothing style, or other distinguishing features.
</details>



## Nature and Capabilities

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

These sections describe what your character is capable of and how they tend to behave.

- **Species**
- **Bloodline**  
  If applicable, include known lineage or notable ancestry.
- **Traits**  
  Personality traits, strengths, flaws, habits, or quirks.
- **Demeanor**  
  How your character typically presents themselves to others.

**Skillset**  
Talents, training, or areas of competence. These should align with your whitelist and in-game progression.

**Occupations**  
Past or present professions, roles, or long-term pursuits.
</details>



## Magic and Combat (Optional)

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

Only include what is relevant to your character.

- **Magical Abilities**  
  Broad descriptions of magical aptitude or themes, not mechanical advantages.
- **Magic Schools**  
  Disciplines your character studies or favors.
- **Weapons**  
  Preferred weapons or tools, if any.
- **Familiars**  
  Bound creatures, companions, or summoned entities, if applicable.
</details>



## Background and History

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

This section is optional and can be as short or long as you like.

**Background**  
A brief overview of your character’s past, focusing on events that shape who they are now. You do not need to include a full life story.

Remember: unresolved questions, gaps in history, and unknowns are completely acceptable and often lead to better roleplay.
</details>



## Final Notes

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

There is no “correct” way to make a character profile.

Some players write a single paragraph. Others enjoy detailed entries. Do what feels right for you and your character.

If you are unsure whether something fits the setting, or if you want help refining a profile, staff are always happy to assist.
</details>

# Citizenship System
## Overview
The citizenship system is a new feature for Season 3 that allows players whitelisting Vardoran-native characters to choose a home faction at character creation. Citizens receive a faction tag that prevents NPCs belonging to their chosen faction from aggroing on them automatically, opening up roleplay and gameplay opportunities that simply weren't available before.

This is a mechanics and flavor system. It is not admin-run, and it does not come with attached plotlines, NPC characters controlled by admins, or scheduled events. What players do with the access and the social context it provides is entirely up to them.
## Who Can Use It
Citizenship is primarily intended for human characters, as humans make up the general native population of Vardoran and the settlements involved are predominantly human communities. 

Whether other races may also have access is something that will be determined as we get closer to the season. This guide will be updated accordingly as those decisions are made.
## Available Factions
The available factions will be confirmed and listed when Season 3 lore and info for whitelists is made public. The list may grow over time as we expand the system.

As a confirmed example, Brighthaven in Silverlight will be one of the available options.

Brighthaven is the heart of Church of Luminance territory. A citizen there may worship Luminance or simply live within the city's influence, working the grape fields or trading in the city as part of their daily life. The faction separation principle described below applies here as with all others: a Brighthaven citizen is not part of the Church of Luminance's armed forces.

**Please do not plan your character around a specific faction until that information is released, as the full list has not been finalized.**
## What Citizenship Does and Does Not Give You
**What it gives you:**
- A faction tag aligned to your chosen settlement, preventing those NPCs from aggroing on you automatically.
- Social and narrative context for your character's background and daily life.
- Access to custom merchants placed in each settlement area, providing alternate means of earning and spending currency and potentially acquiring equipment.

**What it does not give you:**
- Membership in the military or governing body of your faction. A Brighthaven citizen is not part of the Church of Luminance's armed forces.
- Political authority or influence over NPC factions or their behavior.
- Admin support, scheduled events, or plotlines attached to your citizenship. These factions are not admin-run and we will not be running content through them.

If you hit an NPC from your allied faction, they will attack you normally. The tag prevents automatic aggro, not retaliation.
## Expectations
This system is being rolled out after internal testing last season. The goal is to give players new ways to play and new hooks for character backgrounds and daily roleplay without requiring admin involvement to make that interesting.

Players should come into this with a clear understanding that citizenship is a low-power, low-risk flavor choice. Your character is an average person living in their corner of Vardoran. The interesting story that comes out of that is yours to build.

# Rituals Overview

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

Rituals are the act of manipulating the flow of magic through a space to create an effect. The objects, their positions, the hand motions, words spoken, frame of mind, everything in the working space contributes to the flow of magic.

We have a system for determining how difficult a ritual is to complete, and we are not sharing the details of that system. Our goal is for you to try things out, fail forward, and learn how to do it better for the future. If a ritual fails, we will do our best to give you insight into why it failed.

<span class="text-warm">All rituals must be submitted as an RPO ticket. There is no way to perform a ritual without checking in with us first.</span>

</details>

## Roles in a Ritual

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

A successful ritual requires the right combination of people. There are three roles a character can fill.

**Magical School Specialty**

<span class="text-accent">Channeling magic is the domain of Magical School Specialties.</span> If a ritual requires Blood magic, you or someone helping you must have Blood Magic Specialty. If it requires Storm and Chaos magic, you need Specialty in both across the people running the ritual.

**Ritualism Specialty**

Ritualism Specialty is separate from Magical School Specialties. It represents knowledge and experience in constructing and managing ritual spaces. It is not about channeling magic. The Ritualism Specialty stacks on top of Magical School Specialties.

A character can have both Ritualism and a Magical School Specialty. They are not mutually exclusive.

Think of it this way. Magical School Specialties can start the fire. Ritualism Specialty can add fuel to the flames. <span class="text-warm">People who only have Ritualism will not be able to do rituals by themselves.</span> They are a flex role and will need help from someone with the right Magical School Specialty.

**Magical Batteries**

We do not want to block players from participating in rituals just because they lack a Specialty.

Characters who have no magical Specialty but can still use the type of magic at hand are welcome to be magical batteries. <span class="text-accent">You channel your magic into the ritual space, and the ritual controllers use that power to help the ritual along.</span>

Batteries can provide any magic school their character can channel. For example, a human cannot provide Blood magic because they cannot channel Blood magic. A werewolf cannot provide Chaos magic because they cannot channel Chaos magic.

How much a battery helps is not something we will share publicly.

</details>

## What Affects a Ritual

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

<span class="text-accent">There is no single correct way to perform a ritual.</span> Rituals are meant to be flexible, creative means to achieve things. How a ritual is performed is generally up to you. The aspects that are more set in stone are what magical schools are required in order to achieve the effect you want.

There are four main factors we take into consideration when determining how effective a ritual will be.

- **Ritual Space.** The location and setup where the ritual is performed.
- **Ritual Design.** The specific methods, symbols, and procedures used.
- **Material Components.** Physical items consumed or arranged for the ritual. You should expect us to ask for materials and components.
- **Skills of Those Involved.** The Specialties and experience of everyone participating.

</details>

## Failure and Consequences

<details open>
<summary>Click to expand or collapse</summary>

<span class="text-warm">Not every ritual will succeed.</span> When a ritual fails, here is what you can expect.

- You lose the materials and time you put into the ritual.
- You receive insight into why the ritual failed.
- The ritual does not complete as intended.

In some cases, depending on how dangerous the ritual is, the ritual might still complete but do something unintended. Admins have discretion to apply unexpected outcomes when a ritual goes out of control. The risk of this increases with the power of the ritual.

</details>
